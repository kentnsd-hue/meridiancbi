CBI Processing Times Compared: How Long Does It Really Take to Get a Second Passport in 2026?

September 02, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

One of the first questions every prospective applicant asks is deceptively simple: "How long will this take?"

It's also one of the most important. Whether you're restructuring your business ahead of a tax deadline, planning an international relocation, or simply want the security of a Plan B passport before geopolitical uncertainty escalates further, timing matters.

The problem? Processing times vary dramatically from program to program — and the numbers you'll find on government websites rarely tell the full story. Delays, due diligence backlogs, document requests, and seasonal volume can all stretch a timeline that looked straightforward on paper.

At Meridian Advisory, we've guided clients through every major CBI and residency-by-investment program on the market. Below is our honest, experience-informed breakdown of processing times across the most popular programs in 2026 — including what the official timelines don't mention.

Program-by-Program Deep Dive

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis — The Gold Standard for Speed

Official timeline: 45–60 days via the Accelerated Application Process (AAP)

Realistic timeline: 2–4 months from submission to passport delivery

St. Kitts has been the benchmark in the CBI industry since 1984, and in 2026, it remains the program that most consistently delivers on its speed promise. The Accelerated Application Process, introduced several years ago, dramatically cut wait times — for an additional government fee.

What affects the timeline:

  • The standard (non-accelerated) track can take 3–6 months
  • Incomplete documentation is the single biggest cause of delays
  • Dual applications (spouse, dependents) add minor administrative time but are processed concurrently

Best for: Applicants who need a passport quickly and are willing to pay a premium for speed.

🇬🇩 Grenada — The Strategic Choice (with E-2 Treaty Access)

Official timeline: 90–120 days

Realistic timeline: 4–6 months

Grenada consistently processes within its stated window, though passport printing and delivery can add a few weeks beyond the approval date. What makes Grenada unique isn't just speed — it's the E-2 Treaty Investor Visa access to the United States, making it the only Caribbean CBI program with a direct pathway to living and working in the U.S.

What affects the timeline:

  • Due diligence is thorough; applicants from higher-risk jurisdictions may experience additional review
  • Real estate investment options tend to take slightly longer than the donation route due to property conveyancing

Best for: Entrepreneurs and investors who want Caribbean citizenship and a route to the U.S. market.

🇩🇲 Dominica — Affordable and Efficient

Official timeline: 90 days

Realistic timeline: 3–5 months

Dominica has quietly become one of the most efficient CBI programs in the Caribbean. Its government has invested heavily in processing infrastructure, and the results show. It's also the most affordable program in the region, making it a strong option for individuals focused on value.

What affects the timeline:

  • Dominica's due diligence is handled by multiple international firms, which is thorough but generally doesn't cause excessive delays
  • Hurricane season (June–November) has occasionally impacted government office operations in past years, though digital processing has largely mitigated this

Best for: Cost-conscious applicants who want a reliable passport without a six-figure premium.

🇦🇬 Antigua & Barbuda — Family-Friendly Processing

Official timeline: 90–120 days

Realistic timeline: 4–6 months

Antigua's program is particularly attractive for larger families, as its pricing structure can be more favorable for applicants with multiple dependents. Processing is generally predictable, though the program has undergone several policy updates in recent years that applicants should be aware of.

What affects the timeline:

  • The five-day residency requirement (within the first five years) is minimal but must be planned for
  • University-age dependents (18–30) can be included, though additional documentation is required

Best for: Families seeking citizenship together with a program designed to accommodate larger applications.

🇻🇺 Vanuatu — The Fastest Passport on Earth

Official timeline: 30–60 days

Realistic timeline: 1–3 months

If pure speed is the priority, Vanuatu stands alone. Some applicants have received citizenship approval in as few as three weeks. The Pacific island nation's Development Support Program (DSP) is streamlined, digital-first, and designed for rapid processing.

The trade-offs:

  • Vanuatu's passport provides visa-free access to roughly 100 destinations — strong, but not at the level of Caribbean programs (140+) or Malta (180+)
  • The program has faced periodic international scrutiny, and applicants should ensure they work with a reputable advisory firm to navigate any reputational considerations
  • No access to EU or U.S. treaty benefits

Best for: Applicants who need a second passport urgently — for business continuity, travel flexibility, or personal security.

🇲🇹 Malta — The European Gold Standard

Official timeline: 12–14 months (after 12-month residency requirement, or 36 months for reduced investment)

Realistic timeline: 14–18 months (via the expedited 12-month residency route)

Malta's Exceptional Investor Naturalisation (MEIN) program is the only legitimate pathway to full EU citizenship by investment in 2026. That prestige comes with a longer timeline and a significantly higher price tag.

Why it takes longer:

  • Malta requires genuine residency — 12 months minimum (or 36 months for a lower contribution threshold)
  • Due diligence is among the most rigorous in the world, involving multi-agency international checks
  • The application is reviewed by a dedicated government agency, and approval is not guaranteed — rejection rates are meaningfully higher than Caribbean programs

What you get for the wait: A passport with visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 180+ countries, full EU rights (live, work, study anywhere in the EU), and arguably the most respected CBI passport in the world.

Best for: Ultra-high-net-worth individuals who value EU access, prestige, and long-term strategic planning over speed.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa — The Long Game

Official timeline: 12–18 months for initial residency permit

Realistic timeline to citizenship: 5+ years

Portugal is not a CBI program — it's a residency-by-investment program with a pathway to citizenship after five years. We include it here because it remains one of the most requested programs among our clients, particularly those interested in EU residency, Portuguese tax benefits (especially the former NHR regime's successor frameworks), and eventual EU citizenship.

What to know in 2026:

  • The real estate investment route was eliminated in 2023; the primary pathway is now through qualifying investment funds (minimum €500,000)
  • Processing times for the initial Golden Visa have improved somewhat but remain subject to Portugal's immigration service (AIMA) backlogs
  • The five-year clock to citizenship eligibility begins when your residency permit is issued, not when you submit your application
  • Minimal physical presence is required (seven days in the first year, 14 days in subsequent two-year periods)

Best for: Individuals with a five-to-seven-year planning horizon who want EU citizenship through a well-established, respected program.

🇹🇷 Türkiye — Real Estate Route with Volatility

Official timeline: 90–120 days

Realistic timeline: 4–8 months

Türkiye offers citizenship through a $400,000 real estate investment, and on paper, the processing timeline is competitive. In practice, the timeline varies more widely than any other program on this list.

What causes the variance:

  • Property valuation and title deed transfer processes can introduce delays
  • Government processing speeds have fluctuated with political and administrative changes
  • Military service obligations may apply to male applicants of certain ages and must be resolved

Best for: Investors who want a tangible real estate asset alongside citizenship, with access to a large, dynamic economy and visa-free travel to 110+ countries.

What the Official Timelines Don't Tell You

After years of guiding clients through these programs, here are the hidden factors that actually determine how long your application takes:

1. Document Preparation Is Usually the Bottleneck

Most applicants underestimate how long it takes to gather apostilled documents, certified translations, police clearance certificates from every country they've lived in, and detailed financial records. For complex profiles — multiple nationalities, businesses in several jurisdictions, prior visa refusals — this phase alone can take 4–8 weeks.

Our advice: Start document gathering before you've made a final program decision.

2. Due Diligence Depth Varies Enormously

Caribbean programs use established third-party due diligence firms. Malta conducts its own intelligence-grade checks. The depth of your background review directly impacts timeline — and if you have a complex corporate structure, prior litigation, or connections to politically sensitive regions, expect additional time.

3. Your Advisory Firm Matters More Than You Think

A significant portion of processing delays originate not from governments, but from poorly prepared applications. Missing documents, incorrectly completed forms, and inadequate supporting narratives all trigger requests for additional information (RFIs), which can add weeks or months to any program.

Working with an experienced advisory firm doesn't just reduce stress — it materially reduces processing time.

4. Post-Approval ≠ Passport in Hand

Approval is not the finish line. After approval, there's typically a waiting period for passport printing, shipping, and — in some programs — an in-person oath of allegiance or biometric enrollment. This can add 2–6 weeks to any quoted timeline.

Choosing the Right Program Based on Your Timeline

| Your Situation | Recommended Program(s) |

|---|---|

| I need a passport within 90 days | Vanuatu, St. Kitts (Accelerated) |

| I can wait 4–6 months for a stronger passport | Grenada, Dominica, Antigua |

| I want U.S. market access | Grenada (E-2 Treaty) |

| I want EU citizenship and can plan long-term | Malta (14–18 months), Portugal (5+ years) |

| I want real estate + citizenship | Türkiye, Grenada, Antigua |

| Budget is my primary constraint | Dominica |

The Bottom Line

There is no single "best" CBI program — only the best program for your specific situation, timeline, and objectives. A crypto founder liquidating assets ahead of a regulatory change has very different needs than a family planning a multi-year European relocation.

What every applicant has in common: the earlier you start the process, the more options you have.
