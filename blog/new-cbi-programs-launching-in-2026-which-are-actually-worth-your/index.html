New CBI Programs Launching in 2026: Which Are Actually Worth Your Investment?

September 20, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | Updated June 2026

The citizenship by investment landscape is shifting faster than ever. As some legacy programs tighten their requirements and others close entirely, a new wave of countries is entering the market — each hoping to attract high-net-worth individuals with compelling offers.

But not every new program deserves your attention. Some are genuinely transformative opportunities. Others are poorly structured, politically unstable, or simply not worth the risk.

At Meridian Advisory, we've spent months analyzing the programs emerging in 2026. Here's our honest breakdown of what's launching, what's worth considering, and what you should approach with caution.

The State of CBI in 2026: A Market in Transition

Before diving into the new entrants, let's set the stage.

The Caribbean programs — long the gold standard for speed and simplicity — are undergoing significant reform. The CBI Memorandum of Agreement signed by Caribbean nations has introduced stricter due diligence requirements, higher minimum investment thresholds, and greater international oversight. St. Kitts & Nevis and Grenada remain strong, but they're no longer the bargain they once were.

Meanwhile, European residency-to-citizenship pathways are tightening. Portugal's Golden Visa has shifted away from real estate in major cities. Malta remains one of the most prestigious programs globally but comes with a price tag and timeline to match.

Into this gap, several new or restructured programs have emerged in 2026. Let's evaluate them.

1. Malaysia — The New Residency-to-Citizenship Pathway

Status: Officially restructured in early 2026

Malaysia has quietly become one of the most interesting stories in global mobility this year. Building on its long-running Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) program, the government introduced a new premium tier that creates a clearer, more defined pathway from long-term residency toward permanent residency and, eventually, naturalization.

Why It's Worth Watching

  • Strategic location in Southeast Asia with access to ASEAN markets
  • Low cost of living relative to quality of life
  • Favorable tax regime — foreign-sourced income remains largely untaxed for residents
  • Strong infrastructure, modern healthcare, and English widely spoken

The Caveats

This is not a fast-track citizenship program. You're looking at a multi-year residency commitment before citizenship is even on the table. It's best suited for individuals who genuinely want a base in Asia and are willing to play the long game.

Meridian's Verdict: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Excellent for long-term planning and Asia-Pacific access. Not ideal if speed is your priority.

2. Kenya — East Africa's Ambitious New Entrant

Status: Program framework announced Q1 2026

Kenya made headlines earlier this year by announcing a formal investment migration framework aimed at attracting foreign capital into infrastructure, technology, and agriculture. While the full legislative details are still being finalized, the initial structure suggests a residency-by-investment program with a potential naturalization pathway after 5–7 years.

Why It's Interesting

  • Nairobi is a major tech and financial hub — often called "Silicon Savannah"
  • Access to the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), a market of 1.3 billion people
  • Relatively low investment thresholds compared to European or Caribbean programs
  • Growing international credibility and economic momentum

The Caveats

The program is still in its infancy. Regulatory frameworks, due diligence standards, and processing infrastructure need to mature. Early applicants may face unpredictable timelines and evolving requirements.

Meridian's Verdict: ⭐⭐⭐ — High potential, but wait for the regulatory framework to solidify before committing capital. One to monitor closely.

3. Georgia — From Digital Nomad Haven to Investment Migration Player

Status: Enhanced investment residency program launched mid-2026

Georgia has been a darling of the location-independent community for years, thanks to its low taxes, affordable living, and welcoming visa policies. In 2026, the government formalized a more robust investment residency program with clearer pathways to permanent residency and eventual citizenship.

Why It's Worth Considering

  • Territorial tax system — only Georgian-sourced income is taxed
  • No wealth tax, no inheritance tax
  • Rapid company formation and business-friendly environment
  • Strategic position between Europe and Asia
  • Investment minimums remain relatively accessible

The Caveats

Georgian citizenship, while increasingly respected, doesn't carry the same visa-free travel weight as Caribbean or European passports. The Schengen visa-free access is a plus, but it's not an EU passport.

Meridian's Verdict: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Outstanding value proposition for entrepreneurs and remote business owners. The tax structure alone makes it worthy of serious consideration.

4. Montenegro — The EU Accession Wildcard

Status: Revised special investment program under review in 2026

Montenegro's CBI program had a rocky history — launched, paused, and debated multiple times. In 2026, with EU accession negotiations advancing, Montenegro is revisiting its investment migration offering with a structure designed to satisfy EU scrutiny while still attracting foreign capital.

Why It's a Potential Game-Changer

  • If Montenegro joins the EU (potentially by 2028–2030), early investors could hold an EU passport obtained at a fraction of Malta's cost
  • Beautiful Adriatic coastline driving real estate value
  • Small, navigable bureaucracy
  • NATO member, adding geopolitical stability

The Caveats

This is speculative. EU accession is not guaranteed, and the program's final structure may change significantly. There's also the question of whether existing CBI-granted citizenships would be honored post-accession — a genuine legal gray area.

Meridian's Verdict: ⭐⭐⭐ — The EU accession angle is tantalizing, but this is higher-risk than established programs. Only suitable for investors comfortable with uncertainty and a long time horizon.

5. Expanded Caribbean Options: Antigua & Barbuda and Dominica Refresh

Status: Restructured programs active in 2026

While not entirely "new," both Antigua & Barbuda and Dominica have rolled out significant updates to their CBI offerings in 2026 in response to the regional MOU reforms. These refreshed programs feature:

  • Enhanced due diligence (which actually increases passport credibility)
  • New sustainable development fund options
  • Revised real estate investment tiers
  • Faster processing for qualifying applicants

Why the Refresh Matters

The tighter standards are a feature, not a bug. Programs with rigorous vetting produce passports that maintain — and often gain — visa-free access. In a world where some CBI passports are losing travel privileges, the Caribbean nations that invest in compliance are positioning themselves for long-term value.

Meridian's Verdict: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The Caribbean remains the benchmark for speed, simplicity, and value. The 2026 reforms make these programs more credible, not less.

Programs We'd Approach With Caution in 2026

Not every new entrant deserves your capital. We're seeing several programs emerge from jurisdictions with:

  • Weak rule of law or unstable political environments
  • Unclear regulatory frameworks with no independent oversight
  • Limited visa-free travel value
  • Passports that may actually trigger additional scrutiny at borders rather than reducing it

We won't name and shame here, but if a program sounds too good to be true — ultra-low investment, instant citizenship, no due diligence — it almost certainly is. A second passport is only valuable if the world's governments respect it.

Our rule of thumb: If a program doesn't have transparent government backing, independent due diligence, and a credible track record (or credible framework for new programs), walk away.

How to Decide: A Framework for Choosing the Right Program

With so many options on the table, the right choice depends entirely on your specific goals. Ask yourself:

| Your Priority | Best Fit in 2026 |

|---|---|

| Speed to citizenship | St. Kitts & Nevis, Grenada, Dominica |

| EU access (current) | Malta, Portugal Golden Visa |

| EU access (speculative/future) | Montenegro |

| Tax optimization | Georgia, Malaysia |

| African market access | Kenya (once formalized) |

| Overall passport strength | Malta, Portugal |

| Lowest investment threshold | Dominica, Georgia |

| Long-term Asia-Pacific base | Malaysia |

The Bottom Line

2026 is one of the most dynamic years in the history of investment migration. The landscape is evolving rapidly, and the decisions you make now could have profound implications for your family's mobility, tax position, and security for decades to come.

But more options also means more complexity. The difference between a life-changing investment and a costly mistake often comes down to getting the right advice before you commit.
