Second Citizenship for Your Entire Family: A Complete Guide to CBI Programs That Include Parents and Siblings

October 06, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

For high-net-worth individuals exploring citizenship by investment, one question comes up more than almost any other: "Can I include my family?"

The answer is yes — but which family members qualify depends entirely on which program you choose. And the differences are significant.

While nearly every CBI program allows you to include a spouse and minor children, the real differentiator is how each program treats parents, grandparents, adult siblings, and extended dependents. For families who want to move together — or simply want the peace of mind that comes with a shared Plan B — this is where the details matter.

Let's break it down.

Why Family Inclusion Matters in CBI

Citizenship by investment isn't just a personal asset. It's a generational one.

A second passport can mean:

  • Visa-free travel for aging parents who face restrictive travel barriers on their current passport
  • Education access for children and younger siblings in global markets
  • Healthcare options in stable jurisdictions
  • Estate and succession planning across borders
  • Security and stability during political or economic uncertainty at home

When you're investing $100,000 to $1 million+ in a second citizenship, the ability to extend that protection to the people you care about most isn't a nice-to-have. It's often the deciding factor.

Program-by-Program Breakdown: Who Can You Include?

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis

One of the most family-friendly CBI programs in the world.

St. Kitts has long been the gold standard for citizenship by investment, and its family inclusion policies are among the most generous available in 2026.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 31 (if financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents and grandparents aged 55+
  • ✅ Siblings of the main applicant (under 30, unmarried, with no children, and financially dependent)

Investment minimum: Starting at $250,000 (Sustainable Island State Contribution — SISC) for a family of up to four. Additional dependents incur supplementary fees.

Why it stands out: The inclusion of siblings is relatively rare across CBI programs and makes St. Kitts particularly attractive for applicants from cultures where extended family structures are the norm. The program has also undergone significant due diligence reforms in recent years, strengthening passport credibility globally.

Processing time: Approximately 2–4 months.

🇬🇩 Grenada

The gateway to the U.S. — with strong family provisions.

Grenada remains uniquely positioned as the only Caribbean CBI program whose citizens are eligible for the U.S. E-2 Treaty Investor Visa, making it the program of choice for families with American business ambitions.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30 (if enrolled in higher education and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents and grandparents aged 55+ (financially dependent)
  • ✅ Siblings of the main applicant (under 18, unmarried, and financially dependent)

Investment minimum: Starting at $235,000 (National Transformation Fund) for a family of four.

Why it stands out: The E-2 visa pathway is the headline, but Grenada's inclusion of dependent parents makes it a compelling option for families looking to relocate elderly relatives to a jurisdiction with better healthcare access and quality of life. The sibling inclusion is more restrictive than St. Kitts (under 18 only), but still available.

Processing time: Approximately 3–5 months.

🇲🇹 Malta

Europe's only true CBI program — with a premium price and premium benefits.

Malta's Exceptional Investor Naturalisation (MEIN) program grants full EU citizenship, which means freedom to live, work, and travel across all 27 EU member states plus the Schengen Area. For families, this is transformative.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children (including adult children who are financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents and grandparents (financially dependent)
  • ❌ Siblings are not typically included

Investment minimum: The total outlay typically ranges from €690,000 to over €1 million, comprising a non-refundable contribution, a property purchase or rental commitment, and a philanthropic donation.

Why it stands out: This is the only program on this list that results in EU citizenship — a passport that ranks among the top 5–10 globally for visa-free access. The ability to include dependent parents and adult children makes it a genuine multi-generational investment. However, the absence of sibling inclusion and the significantly higher cost and longer timeline (12–36 months including a residency requirement) make this a program suited to families with larger budgets and longer planning horizons.

Processing time: 12–36 months (includes a mandatory residency period of 12 or 36 months depending on contribution level).

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa

A residency-to-citizenship pathway with excellent family reach.

Portugal's Golden Visa isn't technically a CBI program — it's a residency by investment program that can lead to citizenship after five years. But its family reunification provisions make it one of the most comprehensive options for extended families.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse or partner
  • ✅ Minor children
  • ✅ Adult children (if enrolled in education and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Dependent parents of either the main applicant or spouse
  • ❌ Siblings are not included under the golden visa, but may be eligible through separate family reunification channels under Portuguese immigration law

Investment minimum: Starting at €500,000 (fund-based investments following the 2023 reforms that removed direct real estate purchases in high-density areas).

Why it stands out: Portugal offers something the Caribbean programs cannot: a path to EU citizenship and an EU passport at a lower price point than Malta, albeit with a longer timeline. The inclusion of dependent parents of both the applicant and their spouse is a notable advantage. Portuguese citizenship also grants access to Brazil and other Lusophone nations with favorable residency terms.

Processing time: Golden Visa residency approval in 6–12 months; citizenship eligibility after 5 years.

🇦🇬 Antigua and Barbuda

A cost-effective Caribbean option with solid family coverage.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30 (financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents and grandparents aged 55+ (financially dependent)
  • ✅ Siblings of the main applicant (under 18, unmarried, and financially dependent)

Investment minimum: Starting at $230,000 (National Development Fund) for a family of four.

Why it stands out: Antigua requires the main applicant and dependents to spend a minimum of 5 days in Antigua within the first 5 years — a minimal but notable residency requirement. Family inclusion terms closely mirror Grenada's, but without the U.S. E-2 visa advantage.

Processing time: Approximately 3–5 months.

🇩🇲 Dominica

The most affordable Caribbean CBI — but limited sibling inclusion.

Eligible dependents include:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30 (if financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents and grandparents aged 55+ (financially dependent)
  • ❌ Siblings are not eligible

Investment minimum: Starting at $200,000 (Economic Diversification Fund) for a family of four.

Why it stands out: Dominica consistently ranks as the most cost-effective CBI program and has won industry awards for its due diligence and governance. However, the absence of sibling inclusion makes it less suitable for extended family applications.

Processing time: Approximately 2–4 months.

Key Considerations for Families

1. "Financially Dependent" Has a Specific Meaning

Every program that includes parents, adult children, or siblings requires proof that these individuals are financially dependent on the main applicant. This typically means providing bank statements, affidavits, and documentation showing that the dependent does not have independent means of support. Work with an experienced advisor to prepare this documentation correctly — incomplete dependency proof is one of the most common causes of application delays.

2. Costs Scale With Family Size

Each additional dependent adds to your total investment. Government fees, due diligence fees, passport fees, and processing fees all multiply. A single applicant investing in St. Kitts may spend $250,000, but a family of six could be looking at $300,000–$350,000+. Always model the total cost per family member before committing.

3. Future-Proofing Matters

Some programs allow you to add dependents after the fact — for example, a future spouse or a newborn child. Others require a new application entirely. If your family situation is likely to change (marriage, new children, elderly parents becoming dependent), discuss this with your advisor upfront.

4. Due Diligence Applies to Every Family Member

Every adult dependent will undergo background checks, and any red flags — even minor ones — can delay or jeopardize the entire application. Full transparency with your advisory team from day one is essential.

5. Programs Change

CBI program rules are updated frequently. Age limits, investment thresholds, and eligible dependent categories have all shifted across multiple programs in the last 24 months alone. What was accurate in 2024 may not apply in 2026. Always verify current terms with a qualified advisor before making decisions.

So, Which Program Is Best for Your Family?

There's no universal answer — but here's a general framework:

  • If sibling inclusion is a priority → St. Kitts offers the broadest sibling eligibility (under 30)
  • If you want a U.S. business pathway for the whole family → Grenada + E-2 visa
  • If EU access is the goal and budget allows → Malta for speed; Portugal for value
  • If cost efficiency matters most → Dominica (but no siblings) or Antigua (siblings under 18)
  • If you have dependent parents on both sides of the family → Portugal is uniquely generous here

Next Steps

Every family's situation is different — and the best CBI strategy depends on your nationality, family structure, business interests, travel needs, and long-term goals.
