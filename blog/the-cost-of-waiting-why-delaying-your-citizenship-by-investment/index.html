The Cost of Waiting: Why Delaying Your Citizenship by Investment Application Could Be Your Most Expensive Decision

November 23, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The Price of Procrastination Is Going Up — Literally

If you've been researching Citizenship by Investment (CBI) programs and thinking, "I'll get to it next year," here's something you need to understand: every year you wait, the price of a second passport climbs higher.

This isn't speculation. It's a pattern backed by over a decade of data. Governments consistently raise investment thresholds, tighten requirements, and — in some cases — shut programs down entirely with little warning.

The question isn't whether CBI programs will get more expensive. It's how much more you'll pay by the time you finally decide to act.

A Decade of Rising Thresholds: The Numbers Don't Lie

Let's look at the exposed price trajectory of some of the world's most popular CBI programs:

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis

The world's longest-running CBI program has undergone several significant price adjustments:

  • 2012: The minimum donation to the Sugar Industry Diversification Foundation (SIDF) stood at $250,000 for a family of four.
  • 2018: Contribution thresholds were restructured, with the Sustainable Island State Contribution (SISC) introduced at $150,000 for a single applicant — but due diligence fees and processing costs pushed the effective total higher.
  • 2023: A major overhaul raised the minimum contribution to $250,000 for a single applicant under the new Sustainable Growth Fund.
  • 2024–2026: Additional due diligence fees, dependent surcharges, and compliance costs have pushed the all-in cost for a family well past $300,000.

A family that applied in 2018 paid roughly 40–50% less than a family applying today for the same passport.

🇬🇩 Grenada

Grenada's program is particularly attractive because it's one of the few Caribbean CBI nations with an E-2 Treaty with the United States — giving citizens a pathway to live and work in the U.S.

  • 2015: The National Transformation Fund (NTF) contribution started at $200,000 for a single applicant.
  • 2023: The minimum contribution was raised to $235,000 for a single applicant.
  • 2024–2026: Combined with rising processing, due diligence, and government fees, the total cost has increased by approximately 25–30% compared to just a few years ago.

And here's the critical risk: Grenada's E-2 Treaty advantage makes it a target for increased demand, which historically gives governments more leverage to raise prices.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa

Portugal offers one of the most dramatic cautionary tales:

  • 2012: Investors could qualify with a €500,000 real estate purchase — including properties in Lisbon and Porto.
  • 2022: Real estate in high-density urban areas (Lisbon, Porto, the Algarve) was removed as a qualifying option.
  • 2023: The government eliminated all real estate investment routes entirely, restricting the program primarily to fund investments of €500,000+.
  • 2026: The program continues but with significantly narrower qualifying criteria and ongoing political debate about its future.

Investors who moved early bought appreciating Lisbon real estate and secured residency. Those who waited lost the option entirely.

🇲🇹 Malta

Malta's program — one of the few that grants citizenship in an EU member state — has always been premium-priced, and the trajectory has been consistently upward:

  • 2014: The original Individual Investor Programme (IIP) required a €650,000 contribution plus real estate.
  • 2020: The program was replaced by the Malta Exceptional Investor Naturalisation (MEIN) policy, raising the effective minimum investment to approximately €690,000–€750,000 (contribution + property + donation).
  • 2026: When you factor in mandatory property holding requirements, government contributions, and philanthropic donations, the total investment regularly exceeds €1 million for a family.

In just over a decade, the cost of Maltese citizenship has increased by more than 50%.

It's Not Just About Price — Programs Disappear

Rising costs are only half the story. The other half is program closures and restrictions that eliminate your options entirely:

| Event | Impact |

|-------|--------|

| Bulgaria (2022) | Shut down its investor residency-to-citizenship pathway under EU pressure. |

| Cyprus (2020) | Closed its CBI program after a corruption scandal — leaving applicants stranded. |

| Portugal (2023) | Eliminated all real estate routes from the Golden Visa. |

| UK Tier 1 Investor Visa (2022) | Closed permanently. |

| EU Regulatory Pressure (ongoing) | The European Commission continues to push member states to restrict or eliminate investment migration programs. |

The trend is undeniable: the global regulatory environment is tightening, not loosening.

Programs that exist today may not exist tomorrow. And when they close, they close fast — often with little transition period for new applicants.

The Hidden Costs of Delay

Beyond the direct price increases, waiting creates several compounding costs that most people don't consider:

1. Opportunity Cost

A second passport unlocks visa-free travel to 140+ countries, access to new banking relationships, and the ability to structure your business internationally. Every month you delay is a month of unrealized mobility and financial flexibility.

2. Tax Planning Windows Close

Governments worldwide are tightening tax regulations. The OECD's global minimum tax framework, expanding CRS (Common Reporting Standard) participation, and evolving substance requirements mean that the tax optimization strategies available today may narrow or vanish in the near future.

3. Processing Times Are Increasing

As programs receive more applications and implement stricter due diligence, processing timelines have lengthened across the board. St. Kitts applications that once took 60 days now routinely take 90–120+ days. Malta's pathway requires a minimum 12–36 month residency before naturalization. Starting later means finishing even later.

4. Currency and Market Risk

If your wealth is concentrated in a single currency or economy, geopolitical instability, inflation, or capital controls could reduce your purchasing power — making the same program more expensive in real terms even if the sticker price doesn't change.

Who's Most at Risk from Waiting?

Based on our work with hundreds of clients at Meridian Advisory, the profiles most vulnerable to the cost of delay include:

  • Tech Founders & Entrepreneurs approaching a liquidity event — post-exit tax planning requires citizenship to be in place before the event, not after.
  • Crypto Investors holding significant digital asset portfolios in jurisdictions with uncertain or unfavorable regulatory frameworks.
  • Frequent International Travelers whose current passport limits access to key markets.
  • Families with Children — dependent fees increase as children age, and some programs exclude dependents over certain age thresholds.
  • Anyone in a Country with Political or Economic Instability — by the time you need to leave, it's often too late to start a 3–6 month application process.

The Bottom Line: 2026 Is the Cheapest CBI Will Ever Be Again

If the past decade has taught us anything, it's this: CBI programs do not get cheaper over time. They get more expensive, more restrictive, and in some cases, they disappear entirely.

The investment thresholds you see today are almost certainly the lowest they will ever be for these programs. The qualification criteria you meet today may tighten tomorrow.

Waiting doesn't save money. Waiting costs money.
