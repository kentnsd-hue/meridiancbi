How Global Tax Reform Is Driving Unprecedented Demand for Second Citizenships in 2026

October 26, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The global tax landscape is undergoing its most dramatic transformation in a generation. From the OECD's Pillar Two minimum tax framework now in full enforcement across 140+ jurisdictions to sweeping changes in CRS (Common Reporting Standard) data-sharing agreements, the rules that govern how wealth is taxed across borders have fundamentally shifted.

For high-net-worth individuals, entrepreneurs, and investors, the message is clear: the era of passive tax planning is over. And that reality is fueling record-breaking demand for Citizenship by Investment (CBI) programs worldwide.

Here's what's driving the surge — and what it means for anyone serious about protecting their financial future.

The New Tax Reality: What Changed?

1. The OECD Global Minimum Tax Is Now Live

After years of negotiation, the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework's 15% global minimum corporate tax is no longer theoretical. As of 2024–2025, major economies — including the EU, UK, Canada, Japan, South Korea, and Australia — have enacted domestic legislation implementing Pillar Two's GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion) rules.

In 2026, enforcement is tightening. Multinational enterprises with revenues above €750 million are already subject to top-up taxes in jurisdictions where their effective rate falls below 15%. But the ripple effects extend far beyond large corporations:

  • Entrepreneurs planning exits are rethinking where they hold assets pre-sale.
  • Fund managers and investors are reassessing the jurisdictions through which they structure holdings.
  • Digital business owners are recognizing that "tax-neutral" structures are increasingly scrutinized.

2. CRS Expansion and Automatic Data Sharing

The Common Reporting Standard now connects over 120 jurisdictions in automatic exchange of financial account information. In 2026, the updated Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) is bringing digital assets into the same transparency regime.

For crypto investors, DeFi participants, and anyone holding assets across multiple countries, this means:

  • Your bank accounts, brokerage accounts, and now crypto exchange holdings are reported to your tax residence jurisdiction automatically.
  • Offshore accounts are no longer "invisible."
  • Tax residency — not just citizenship — determines your reporting obligations.

3. Wealth Taxes and Exit Taxes Are Proliferating

Several countries have introduced or expanded wealth-related taxes in recent years:

  • Norway increased its wealth tax rate and tightened emigration rules after a high-profile wave of HNWI departures.
  • Spain's Solidarity Tax on large fortunes continues to apply.
  • Canada implemented capital gains inclusion rate increases.
  • The United States continues to enforce its uniquely aggressive citizenship-based taxation system, with exit tax provisions under IRC §877A affecting any covered expatriate.

The trend is unmistakable: governments are casting wider nets, and the cost of inaction is rising.

Why Second Citizenship Has Become a Strategic Necessity

Against this backdrop, Citizenship by Investment is no longer a luxury or a contingency plan. It has become a core component of sophisticated wealth planning — and demand data confirms it.

The Numbers Tell the Story

  • St. Kitts and Nevis, the world's longest-running CBI program, reported a significant increase in applications throughout 2025, with momentum carrying strongly into 2026.
  • Malta's naturalization-by-investment pathway continues to attract European-focused applicants despite its rigorous due diligence, precisely because of its EU membership benefits.
  • Portugal's Golden Visa, restructured to exclude real estate in major cities, still draws substantial interest through its fund investment route — largely for the pathway to EU residency and eventual citizenship it provides.
  • Grenada remains the only Caribbean CBI nation with an E-2 Treaty with the United States, making it uniquely attractive for entrepreneurs who want US market access without the US tax burden.

How Second Citizenship Fits Into Modern Tax Planning

Let's be direct: a second passport is not a tax avoidance tool. Anyone claiming otherwise is either uninformed or being dishonest. Every legitimate CBI program operates within international regulatory frameworks, and Meridian Advisory works exclusively within fully compliant structures.

What a second citizenship does provide is optionality — and in the current environment, optionality is extraordinarily valuable.

Scenario 1: The Tech Founder Pre-Exit

A software founder based in a high-tax European country is two years away from a potential acquisition. By obtaining citizenship in a jurisdiction with no capital gains tax — such as St. Kitts and Nevis — and establishing genuine tax residency there before the liquidity event, the founder can legally reduce their tax exposure by millions.

The key phrase is genuine tax residency. This requires substance: actual relocation, severing of prior tax ties, and meeting the residency requirements of the new jurisdiction. Done properly and with adequate lead time, this is entirely lawful and increasingly common.

Scenario 2: The Crypto Investor

A US-based crypto investor faces citizenship-based taxation — meaning they owe US taxes regardless of where they live. For this individual, a second citizenship alone doesn't change their US obligations. However, it creates the option to renounce US citizenship in the future if they choose, with a second passport ensuring they're never stateless.

For non-US crypto holders, establishing tax residency in a CBI jurisdiction ahead of CARF enforcement can provide a more favorable reporting environment — again, when done with proper legal structuring.

Scenario 3: The Global Family

A family with business interests across three continents uses a Grenadian passport for visa-free travel to 145+ destinations, including the UK and EU Schengen area. The E-2 Treaty access allows one family member to operate a US-based business without triggering US tax residency. Meanwhile, they maintain their primary residence in a territorial tax jurisdiction, paying taxes only on locally sourced income.

This isn't a loophole. It's architecture.

Choosing the Right Program: A Quick Comparison

Not all CBI programs serve the same purpose. Here's a high-level view of the programs we most frequently recommend at Meridian Advisory:

🇰🇳 St. Kitts and Nevis

  • Minimum Investment: $250,000 (Sustainable Island State Contribution) or $325,000+ (real estate)
  • Timeline: 3–6 months
  • Key Benefits: No income tax, no capital gains tax, no wealth tax. Visa-free access to 155+ countries. Longest-running and most established CBI program globally.
  • Best For: Entrepreneurs, investors, and families seeking a well-tested program with strong global recognition.

🇬🇩 Grenada

  • Minimum Investment: $235,000 (National Transformation Fund) or $270,000+ (real estate)
  • Timeline: 4–6 months
  • Key Benefits: E-2 Treaty access to the United States. Visa-free travel to 145+ countries including China. No income tax on foreign earnings.
  • Best For: Entrepreneurs wanting US business access, investors with interests across US, UK, and EU markets.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa

  • Minimum Investment: €500,000 (qualifying investment funds)
  • Timeline: Residency permit in 6–8 months; citizenship eligibility after 5 years
  • Key Benefits: Pathway to EU citizenship and passport. NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) successor regime may offer tax benefits for new residents. Access to all 27 EU member states.
  • Best For: Individuals and families seeking permanent EU access and long-term relocation optionality.

🇲🇹 Malta

  • Minimum Investment: €690,000+ (contribution, property, donation combined)
  • Timeline: 12–36 months (includes mandatory residency period)
  • Key Benefits: EU citizenship and passport. Exceptionally strong passport ranking. English-speaking, stable jurisdiction with robust financial services sector.
  • Best For: Ultra-high-net-worth individuals who want the strongest possible EU passport and are willing to invest time in the process.

The Cost of Waiting

Here's what we tell every prospective client at Meridian Advisory: the biggest risk in CBI planning is delay.

  • Programs change. Portugal eliminated its real estate Golden Visa route in 2023. Countries regularly adjust contribution thresholds, due diligence requirements, and processing times.
  • Tax reform accelerates. CARF, Pillar Two enforcement, and new domestic tax legislation create moving targets. Structures that work today may not be available in 18 months.
  • Processing takes time. Even the fastest CBI programs require 3–6 months. If your liquidity event, relocation, or regulatory deadline is approaching, starting now is starting late.

The individuals who benefit most from second citizenship are invariably those who planned before they urgently needed it.
