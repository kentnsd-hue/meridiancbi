How Global Tax Reform Is Driving Unprecedented Demand for Second Citizenships in 2026

August 15, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The global tax landscape is undergoing its most dramatic transformation in a generation. From the OECD's Pillar Two minimum tax framework now in active enforcement across over 140 jurisdictions, to sweeping changes in residency-based taxation rules in the EU and beyond, high-net-worth individuals and entrepreneurs are facing a new fiscal reality.

The response? A record-breaking surge in applications for Citizenship by Investment (CBI) programs worldwide.

In this post, we break down the key tax reform developments reshaping the global landscape — and why second citizenship has become one of the most strategic tools available to internationally minded individuals.

The New Tax Reality: What Changed?

1. The OECD Global Minimum Tax Is Now a Fact of Life

The Pillar Two framework, which establishes a 15% global minimum corporate tax rate, has moved from policy discussion to enforcement. In 2026, over 40 countries are actively implementing Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Taxes (QDMTTs), and cross-border reporting requirements have tightened significantly.

For founders and business owners who previously structured operations through low-tax jurisdictions, the arbitrage has narrowed. The focus has shifted from where your company is incorporated to where you, as an individual, hold tax residency and citizenship.

2. CRS and Automatic Exchange of Information Have Matured

The Common Reporting Standard (CRS) now covers more than 120 jurisdictions with increasingly sophisticated data-matching capabilities. Banking secrecy, in any meaningful sense, is over. Governments know where your accounts are, what they hold, and whether you've reported them.

This isn't about evasion — it never should have been. But it has made strategic residency and citizenship planning vastly more important. Where you are legally tax resident determines which country has the primary right to tax your global income.

3. Exit Taxes and Departure Levies Are Spreading

Countries including Canada, Australia, Norway, and several EU member states have either introduced or expanded exit taxes — levies imposed on unrealized capital gains when a tax resident departs. The United States has long maintained its expatriation tax under Section 877A, and other nations are now following suit.

The implication is clear: the later you plan, the more expensive it becomes to leave.

4. Citizenship-Based Taxation Proposals Are Gaining Traction

Perhaps the most alarming development for globally mobile individuals: multiple countries are now studying the U.S. model of citizenship-based taxation (CBT). While no major economy beyond the U.S. and Eritrea has formally adopted CBT, serious policy discussions in the EU and Australia signal a potential shift.

For entrepreneurs and investors, the message is unmistakable — securing alternative citizenship before such legislation passes is a form of strategic insurance.

Why Second Citizenship Has Become a Tax Planning Essential

Let's be clear: Citizenship by Investment is not a tax avoidance scheme. It's a legal framework recognized by international law that grants individuals the right to reside, work, and — critically — establish tax residency in a new jurisdiction.

Here's why demand has surged in 2026:

✅ Legitimate Tax Residency Diversification

A second citizenship provides the legal foundation to relocate your tax residency to a jurisdiction with a more favorable fiscal framework. Countries like St. Kitts and Nevis and Grenada levy zero personal income tax on worldwide income. When combined with genuine residency (meeting substance requirements), this represents a fully compliant and legal tax planning structure.

✅ Protection Against Future Legislative Risk

Tax law changes are, by nature, prospective — but they often come with retrospective reporting requirements. Holding a second citizenship before your home country introduces exit taxes, departure restrictions, or CBT gives you options that simply don't exist after the fact.

✅ Asset Protection and Estate Planning Flexibility

Multiple citizenships allow for more sophisticated estate planning, including the ability to hold assets, establish trusts, and domicile entities across jurisdictions with favorable inheritance and wealth transfer rules. In a world of rising wealth taxes — France's IFI, Spain's Solidarity Tax, Norway's increased net wealth levies — this flexibility is invaluable.

✅ Banking and Financial Access

As FATCA and CRS have made banking more complex for certain passport holders, a second citizenship from a well-regarded jurisdiction like Malta or Portugal can simplify the process of opening and maintaining international bank accounts, brokerage accounts, and crypto custody arrangements.

Which CBI Programs Are Best Positioned for Tax-Conscious Investors?

Not all second citizenship programs offer the same fiscal advantages. Here's a snapshot of the most relevant options in 2026:

The Profile of the 2026 CBI Applicant

The individuals seeking second citizenship today look very different from a decade ago. Based on the consultations we conduct daily at Meridian Advisory, the typical 2026 applicant is:

  • A tech founder or SaaS entrepreneur preparing for a liquidity event and seeking to optimize the tax treatment of proceeds
  • A crypto investor who has realized significant gains and faces ambiguous or hostile tax treatment in their home jurisdiction
  • A dual-career family seeking EU residency for education, healthcare, and lifestyle — with tax efficiency as a secondary (but meaningful) benefit
  • A retiring professional seeking to relocate to a zero-tax or low-tax jurisdiction without losing access to global banking and travel
  • A business owner in an unstable political environment who views second citizenship as both a fiscal tool and a personal security measure

The Cost of Waiting

If there is one theme that defines the current environment, it's this: optionality is a depreciating asset.

Every year, more countries tighten exit tax regimes, expand CRS reporting, and narrow the windows for legal tax residency changes. CBI programs themselves are subject to change — the EU continues to pressure Caribbean programs, and several jurisdictions have raised investment thresholds in recent years.

The individuals who benefit most from CBI are those who plan proactively — not reactively.
