CBI Processing Times Compared: How Long Does It Actually Take to Get a Second Passport in 2026?

July 26, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

One of the first questions every prospective applicant asks is deceptively simple: "How long will this take?"

It's also one of the most misunderstood aspects of Citizenship by Investment. Marketing materials promise fast timelines. Governments quote official estimates. But the reality on the ground — with due diligence backlogs, document gathering, and bureaucratic bottlenecks — often tells a different story.

At Meridian Advisory, we've guided clients through every major CBI program. Here's an honest, data-informed breakdown of what to expect in 2026, from application to passport in hand.

\Realistic timelines include document preparation, submission, government processing, and passport issuance — not just the due diligence window.*

Program-by-Program Breakdown

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis: The Gold Standard, Now Faster Than Ever

St. Kitts has been the benchmark in CBI since 1984, and in 2026 it remains one of the most efficient programs available.

What the government says: 45–60 days under the Accelerated Application Process (AAP).

What we see: Most clients receive approval within 60 days of a complete submission. The key word is complete. Document preparation — including certified translations, police clearances from every country of residence, bank reference letters, and medical exams — typically takes 3–6 weeks before you even file.

Total realistic timeline: 2–4 months from engagement to passport.

Why it's fast: St. Kitts invested heavily in modernizing its Citizenship by Investment Unit (CIU). The AAP, which carries an additional processing fee, has genuinely delivered on its speed promise. The program also benefits from decades of institutional experience.

Watch out for: Delays in obtaining police clearances from certain jurisdictions (looking at you, Brazil and Nigeria) can push timelines. Start these documents early.

🇬🇩 Grenada: The Strategic Choice (With Moderate Wait Times)

Grenada occupies a unique position in the CBI landscape — it's the only Caribbean program whose citizens are eligible for the U.S. E-2 Treaty Investor Visa. That single advantage makes it the top choice for clients with American business ambitions.

What the government says: 90–120 days.

What we see: Grenada's processing has been remarkably consistent. Government review typically falls within the 90-day window, though complex applications (large families, applicants with multiple former nationalities) can stretch to 120+ days.

Total realistic timeline: 4–6 months from engagement to passport.

Why choose it despite the longer wait: The E-2 visa eligibility alone justifies the extra weeks. For clients who want Caribbean citizenship and a pathway to live and work in the United States, there is no substitute.

Pro tip: Grenada also offers visa-free access to China — a rarity among CBI programs and a significant advantage for clients with business interests in Asia.

🇩🇲 Dominica: The Quiet Performer

Dominica rarely makes headlines, but it consistently delivers one of the most cost-effective and reasonably paced CBI experiences available.

What the government says: 60–90 days.

What we see: Dominica has been processing within 60–75 days for straightforward applications in 2026. It's not the absolute fastest, but the combination of speed and affordability is hard to beat.

Total realistic timeline: 3–5 months from engagement to passport.

Best for: Budget-conscious applicants who want a reliable Caribbean passport without the premium price tag of St. Kitts. Dominica's passport provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 140+ countries and territories.

🇦🇬 Antigua & Barbuda: Family-Friendly With Moderate Timelines

Antigua is particularly popular with families, thanks to its inclusion of dependent children up to age 30 and dependent parents over 55.

What the government says: 90–120 days.

What we see: Antigua tends to sit at the longer end of its range. Approvals in 100–120 days are common, and passport issuance adds another 2–4 weeks post-approval.

Total realistic timeline: 4–6 months from engagement to passport.

Notable requirement: Antigua requires new citizens to spend a minimum of 5 days in the country within the first 5 years. It's minimal, but worth noting for compliance planning.

🇻🇺 Vanuatu: The Speed Champion

If speed is the single most important factor, Vanuatu is unmatched in 2026.

What the government says: 30–60 days.

What we see: We've seen approvals in as few as 30 days. Passport issuance follows quickly. Vanuatu's Development Support Program (DSP) is designed for velocity.

Total realistic timeline: 1–3 months from engagement to passport.

The trade-offs: Vanuatu's passport offers visa-free access to roughly 100 countries and territories — significantly fewer than Caribbean programs. It does not provide visa-free access to the EU/Schengen zone. The program has also faced periodic scrutiny from international bodies regarding due diligence standards, though recent reforms have strengthened oversight.

Best for: Clients who need a second passport urgently — for travel flexibility, business continuity, or as a bridge while pursuing a more robust program simultaneously.

🇲🇹 Malta: The Premium European Option

Malta's Exceptional Investor Naturalization (MEIN) policy is the only legitimate direct path to EU citizenship through investment. That exclusivity comes with a price — in both money and time.

What the government says: Minimum 12-month residency requirement (which can be reduced to 12 months with a €750,000 contribution, versus 36 months at €600,000).

What we see: From initial application to passport, most clients are looking at 14–20 months on the faster track. Malta's due diligence is among the most rigorous in the world — a four-tier verification process that includes checks by international agencies. This is a feature, not a bug. Malta's thoroughness is precisely what gives its passport credibility.

Total realistic timeline: 14–20 months (12-month track) or 36–42 months (36-month track).

Why it's worth the wait: An EU passport. Full rights to live, work, and do business across all 27 EU member states plus the EEA. Visa-free access to 180+ countries. For clients whose priority is the quality and global power of the passport, Malta is the undisputed leader in CBI.

Important note: Malta caps approvals at approximately 400 per year (excluding dependents). Demand consistently exceeds supply. Early application is strongly recommended.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa: A Marathon, Not a Sprint

Portugal's Golden Visa isn't technically a CBI program — it's a residency-by-investment pathway that can lead to citizenship after meeting specific requirements.

What the government says: Initial residency permit in 12–18 months. Eligibility for citizenship after 5 years of legal residency.

What we see: In 2026, initial Golden Visa approvals are running 12–18 months due to continued high demand, particularly after the program was restructured to focus on fund-based investments (real estate direct investment was largely phased out in 2023). Citizenship applications after the 5-year mark can add another 12–18 months of processing.

Total realistic timeline to citizenship: 6–7+ years.

Why clients still choose it: Portugal offers something no Caribbean program can — EU residency and eventually EU citizenship at a lower cost than Malta, with minimal physical presence requirements (just 7 days per year on average). It's ideal for clients who are planning long-term and want a European base.

🇹🇷 Turkey: Underrated and Improving

Turkey's CBI program, primarily through $400,000 in real estate investment, has matured significantly.

What the government says: 90–120 days.

What we see: Processing has become more predictable in 2026, typically falling in the 90–120 day range for approvals. However, the full cycle — including property acquisition, title deed transfer, and passport issuance — realistically spans 4–8 months.

Total realistic timeline: 4–8 months.

Strategic value: A Turkish passport provides visa-free access to 110+ countries and territories. More importantly, Turkey offers E-2 treaty eligibility with the U.S. (like Grenada) and serves as a business gateway bridging Europe, the Middle East, and Central Asia.

What Actually Causes Delays?

In our experience, the biggest timeline killers are rarely on the government's side. They're on the applicant's side:

1. Incomplete documentation. A missing apostille, an expired police clearance, or a bank reference letter that doesn't meet specifications can set you back weeks. This is the #1 cause of avoidable delays.

2. Multi-jurisdictional backgrounds. Applicants who have lived in 4+ countries face compounding delays as each jurisdiction processes clearance requests at its own pace.

3. Source of funds complexity. Clients with income from crypto, multiple business entities, or inheritance chains require more extensive documentation — and governments take longer to review them.

4. Family size. Applications with 5+ dependents simply take longer to process. Each dependent undergoes individual due diligence.

5. Choosing the wrong program. This sounds obvious, but we regularly speak with clients who spent months pursuing a program that was never the right fit. A 30-minute strategy call at the outset can save months of wasted effort.
