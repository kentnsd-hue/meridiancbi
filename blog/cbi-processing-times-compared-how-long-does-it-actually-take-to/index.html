CBI Processing Times Compared: How Long Does It Actually Take to Get Second Citizenship in 2026?

October 03, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

One of the first questions every client asks us is deceptively simple:

"How long will this take?"

It's the right question. Whether you're seeking a second passport for business mobility, family security, tax planning, or simply optionality in an uncertain world, timing matters. A citizenship that arrives six months from now serves a very different strategic purpose than one that arrives in three years.

The problem? Most of what you'll find online is outdated, vague, or flat-out wrong. Processing times shift constantly as governments adjust staffing, introduce new due diligence protocols, and respond to geopolitical pressures.

So here's the honest, updated breakdown for 2026 — based on what we're actually seeing in our pipeline at Meridian Advisory, not what's printed in a government brochure.

The Detailed Breakdown

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis — The Gold Standard in Speed

Timeline: 60–90 days standard | 45 days accelerated

St. Kitts operates the world's oldest CBI program (established 1984), and its maturity shows. The Citizenship by Investment Unit (CIU) has refined its processes over four decades, and in 2026, it remains one of the fastest and most reliable pathways to a second passport.

The standard route consistently delivers within 60–90 days. For clients who need expedited processing, the Accelerated Application Process (AAP) can deliver approval in as few as 45 days for an additional government fee.

Why it's fast: Institutional experience, dedicated processing staff, and a government that treats CBI revenue as a critical economic pillar.

What can slow it down: Incomplete documentation, complex corporate structures requiring enhanced due diligence, or applicants with ties to higher-risk jurisdictions.

Best for: Clients who need speed combined with a globally respected passport (visa-free access to 150+ destinations, including the UK and Schengen zone).

🇬🇩 Grenada — Fast Processing, Unique U.S. Advantage

Timeline: 90–120 days

Grenada is the only Caribbean CBI country whose citizens are eligible for the U.S. E-2 Treaty Investor Visa. That single fact makes it the most strategically valuable Caribbean passport for anyone with business interests in the United States.

Processing times have held steady in 2026 at 90–120 days, with the government maintaining rigorous but efficient due diligence. We've seen approvals come through in as few as 60 days for straightforward single-applicant cases.

Why it matters beyond speed: The E-2 visa pathway allows Grenadian citizens to live and work in the U.S. by investing in or starting a U.S.-based business — a route not available to citizens of most other CBI countries.

Best for: Entrepreneurs and investors who want Caribbean citizenship and a doorway into the American market.

🇩🇲 Dominica — The Budget-Friendly Workhorse

Timeline: 90–120 days

Dominica offers the lowest entry point among reputable CBI programs at $200,000 for a single applicant donation. Processing times are comparable to Grenada — typically 90–120 days.

The program has strengthened its due diligence protocols significantly in recent years, which has slightly extended average timelines compared to five years ago. But the trade-off is worth it: enhanced scrutiny has improved the passport's international standing and long-term viability.

Best for: Cost-conscious applicants who want a reliable second passport without paying a premium.

🇦🇬 Antigua & Barbuda — Family-Friendly, Competitive Timing

Timeline: 90–120 days

Antigua's program is particularly attractive for families. The government allows inclusion of dependent children, parents, grandparents, and even siblings under certain conditions — one of the most generous family inclusion policies in the Caribbean.

In 2026, processing times remain competitive at 90–120 days. The government also offers a reduced donation option for families of four or more, making the per-person cost highly competitive.

Best for: Families seeking citizenship together with a broad definition of eligible dependents.

🇻🇺 Vanuatu — The Fastest Passport on Earth

Timeline: 30–60 days

If pure speed is the priority, Vanuatu is unmatched. The Development Support Program (DSP) routinely delivers citizenship in 30–60 days, and we've seen cases finalize in as few as 30 days from submission.

The trade-off? Vanuatu's passport, while offering visa-free access to approximately 90+ destinations (including the UK and Schengen), doesn't carry the same breadth of travel freedom as Caribbean or European options. There are also ongoing questions in the international community about the program's due diligence standards, though the government has taken meaningful steps to address these concerns.

Best for: Clients who need a second passport immediately — for travel, safety, or as a bridge while pursuing a longer-term program in parallel.

🇲🇹 Malta — The European Passport (Worth the Wait)

Timeline: 12–14 months

Malta is the only current program that grants full EU citizenship through investment, and the timeline reflects the weight of what's being offered. A Maltese passport provides the right to live, work, and do business in all 27 EU member states, plus visa-free access to 180+ destinations.

The process involves a mandatory 12-month residency period (or 36 months for a lower investment threshold), followed by government review and approval. In practice, from application to passport, expect 12–14 months at the faster investment tier.

Malta's due diligence is among the most thorough in the world. The government conducts four-tier checks, including Interpol and Europol screenings, and rejects a meaningful percentage of applicants.

Investment structure: Malta requires a composite investment — a non-refundable contribution (€600,000–€750,000), a property purchase or rental, and a philanthropic donation. Total outlay typically exceeds €1 million.

Best for: Ultra-high-net-worth individuals who want the most powerful passport money can buy and are willing to invest the time and capital to obtain it.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa — A Marathon, Not a Sprint

Timeline: 18–24 months to residency | 5 years to citizenship eligibility

Portugal's Golden Visa is not a CBI program in the traditional sense — it's a residency-by-investment program that creates a pathway to citizenship after five years. But it remains one of the most popular programs we advise on, especially for clients who value European residency rights and are playing the long game.

Following the 2023 reforms that eliminated real estate as a qualifying investment, the primary route in 2026 is through investment fund subscriptions (minimum €500,000). Initial residency card processing currently takes 18–24 months due to persistent backlogs at SEF's successor agency (AIMA), though the government has been investing in reducing wait times.

After receiving residency, you must maintain it for five years (with minimal physical presence requirements — as few as seven days per year) before applying for citizenship. Portuguese language proficiency (A2 level) is required at the citizenship stage.

Total timeline to passport: 6–7 years realistically.

Best for: Clients who want EU residency now and citizenship eventually, with minimal disruption to their current lifestyle.

🇹🇷 Türkiye — Underrated and Improving

Timeline: 90–180 days

Türkiye's CBI program has matured considerably. For a $400,000 real estate investment (held for three years), applicants can obtain Turkish citizenship — and the passport has been gaining visa-free destinations steadily.

Processing times vary more widely than Caribbean programs (90–180 days) due to the involvement of multiple government agencies, but the trend in 2026 is toward faster processing.

Best for: Investors who want to combine citizenship with tangible real estate assets, particularly in Istanbul's booming market.

What Actually Determines Your Timeline?

The ranges above are real, but where you fall within them depends on several factors:

1. Document Readiness

The single biggest variable. If your birth certificates, police clearances, financial statements, and corporate documents are ready and properly apostilled, you can shave weeks off your timeline. If they're not, expect delays before your application even enters the queue.

2. Complexity of Your Profile

A salaried tech executive with one passport and straightforward finances will process faster than a serial entrepreneur with business interests in twelve countries, multiple nationalities, and complex trust structures. Enhanced due diligence takes longer because it should.

3. Family Size

More applicants on a single application means more due diligence checks, more documents, and more processing time. A family of six will almost always take longer than a solo applicant.

4. Source of Funds

Governments are under enormous international pressure to verify that CBI investment funds are legitimate. If your wealth comes from cryptocurrency gains, you'll need detailed transaction histories and exchange records. If it comes from a business sale, you'll need acquisition documents. Ambiguity here is the most common cause of delays.

5. Geopolitical Factors

Citizens of certain countries face enhanced scrutiny across all programs. This isn't arbitrary — it reflects international compliance standards. If this applies to you, an experienced advisor can help you prepare a more thorough application upfront, which paradoxically can reduce overall processing time.

The Hidden Timeline: Pre-Application Preparation

One thing most comparison articles ignore is the preparation phase — the weeks (sometimes months) before an application is even submitted.

At Meridian Advisory, we typically spend 2–6 weeks with a new client before filing:

  • Week 1: Initial consultation, program selection, and strategy alignment
  • Weeks 2–3: Document collection, apostille coordination, financial documentation
  • Weeks 3–5: Application drafting, review, and quality assurance
  • Week 4–6: Submission and government acknowledgment

This front-end investment in preparation quality is what separates a 60-day approval from a 120-day approval. Governments process clean, complete applications faster. Period.

Our Recommendation: The Parallel Strategy

For clients who can afford it, we increasingly recommend a dual-track approach:

1. Apply for a Caribbean passport now (St. Kitts, Grenada, or Dominica) for immediate global mobility within 60–120 days.

2. Simultaneously begin a European residency program (Portugal or Malta) for long-term strategic positioning.

This gives you the best of both worlds: a second passport in your hand within months, and a European pathway maturing in the background. The two processes run independently and don't interfere with each other.

The Bottom Line

Processing times are important, but they're only one variable in a much larger equation. The right program for you depends on your travel needs, family situation, tax strategy, business interests, and long-term goals.

A passport that arrives in 30 days but doesn't serve your actual needs is worse than one that arrives in 12 months but transforms your global mobility and financial planning.

That's why the first step is always a conversation — not an application.
