Second Citizenship Strategies for SaaS Founders Planning an Exit: What You Need to Know Before the Term Sheet

August 27, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The exit is the finish line every SaaS founder dreams about. But without the right global strategy in place, it can also be the moment you leave millions on the table.

If you're a SaaS founder with ARR north of $5M and acquirers or PE firms circling, you've probably spent thousands of hours optimizing your product, your team, and your cap table. But have you optimized yourself?

The truth is, your personal tax residency and citizenship status may be the single most overlooked variable in your exit strategy — and by the time the LOI hits your inbox, it's often too late to act.

Here's what forward-thinking SaaS founders are doing in 2026 to protect their wealth, expand their options, and approach liquidity events with maximum flexibility.

The Problem: Capital Gains at the Moment of Maximum Value

For U.S.-based founders, long-term federal capital gains rates currently sit at 20%, plus the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax. Layer on state-level taxes — California at 13.3%, New York at 10.9%, Massachusetts at 9% — and your effective tax rate on an exit can exceed 37%.

On a $50 million exit, that's the difference between walking away with $31.5 million and $43 million or more.

For founders based in countries like Canada, the UK, or Australia, the math can be equally punishing, with capital gains inclusion rates and progressive brackets eating into proceeds.

This isn't about evasion. This is about structural planning — the same kind of planning your acquirer's CFO is doing on their side of the deal.

Why Second Citizenship? Why Now?

A second citizenship isn't a magic tax eraser. But it is a foundational building block that unlocks a range of legitimate strategies. Here's what it actually provides:

1. Residency Optionality

A second passport gives you the legal right to reside in a jurisdiction with more favorable tax treatment. Several Caribbean nations — including St. Kitts & Nevis and Grenada — have zero capital gains tax, zero income tax on foreign-sourced earnings, and no wealth tax.

If you establish genuine residency before your exit and properly sever tax ties with your current high-tax jurisdiction, the savings can be transformational.

2. Plan B Flexibility

Geopolitical instability, shifting regulatory environments, and increasing CRS (Common Reporting Standard) complexity mean that having one passport tied to one tax system is a single point of failure. SaaS founders understand redundancy in infrastructure — this is redundancy for your personal life.

3. Investor and Acquirer Confidence

Increasingly, PE firms and strategic acquirers are running deals through international structures. A founder with global mobility and a clean multi-jurisdictional setup can actually simplify deal mechanics, particularly in cross-border acquisitions.

4. Post-Exit Lifestyle Design

After spending a decade building, most founders want freedom. A second citizenship opens up visa-free travel to 140–160+ countries, the ability to bank internationally, and the freedom to live wherever makes sense for your next chapter — whether that's Lisbon, Singapore, or a beach in the Grenadines.

The Top CBI Programs SaaS Founders Are Using in 2026

Not all citizenship-by-investment programs are created equal. Here's how the leading options stack up for a typical SaaS founder planning an exit in the next 12–36 months:

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis

  • Minimum Investment: $250,000 (Sustainable Island State Contribution) or $325,000+ (real estate)
  • Timeline: 3–6 months
  • Key Benefit: The oldest and most established CBI program. Zero income tax, zero capital gains tax, zero inheritance tax. Strong reputation and due diligence standards.
  • Best for: Founders who want speed and credibility.

🇬🇩 Grenada

  • Minimum Investment: $235,000 (National Transformation Fund) or $270,000+ (real estate)
  • Timeline: 4–6 months
  • Key Benefit: The only Caribbean CBI nation with an E-2 Treaty with the United States. This means Grenadian citizens can apply for a U.S. E-2 Investor Visa — critical for founders who want to maintain U.S. market access while relocating their tax residency.
  • Best for: Founders who want to leave the U.S. tax net but keep a foot in the market.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa

  • Minimum Investment: €500,000 (qualifying fund investment)
  • Timeline: 12–18 months for residency; pathway to citizenship in ~5 years
  • Key Benefit: EU residency and eventual citizenship. Access to Portugal's Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) successor regime, which can offer favorable treatment on certain foreign income streams. Gateway to all 27 EU member states.
  • Best for: Founders with a longer runway who want EU access and lifestyle.

🇲🇹 Malta

  • Minimum Investment: €690,000–€840,000+ (combination of contribution, property, and donation)
  • Timeline: 14–36 months
  • Key Benefit: EU citizenship — one of the only programs that grants an actual EU passport. Exceptional for founders who want permanent, inheritable EU access.
  • Best for: Founders with larger exits who want the strongest possible passport.

The Critical Timeline: When to Start

This is where most founders get it wrong. Citizenship and residency planning is not a post-exit activity. It's a pre-exit strategy.

Here's a realistic timeline:

| Months Before Exit | Action |

|---|---|

| 24–36 months | Begin CBI application. Engage tax counsel in both your current and target jurisdictions. |

| 18–24 months | Obtain second citizenship. Begin establishing genuine ties to new jurisdiction (banking, housing, social connections). |

| 12–18 months | Formally transition tax residency. File appropriate departure returns. Sever ties with prior jurisdiction per legal requirements. |

| 6–12 months | Operate and live from new jurisdiction. Ensure all substance requirements are met. |

| 0 months | Close the exit from a position of optimized personal structure. |

The key phrase here is "genuine ties." Tax authorities worldwide — including the IRS, HMRC, and the CRA — are sophisticated. Simply obtaining a passport is not sufficient. You need to demonstrate substantive relocation. This is why working with experienced advisors who understand both the immigration and tax dimensions is essential.

Common Mistakes SaaS Founders Make

❌ Waiting until the LOI is signed. By then, you're locked into your current tax position. Most restructuring needs 12–24 months of lead time.

❌ Confusing citizenship with tax residency. A passport alone doesn't change your tax obligations. You need to pair CBI with a deliberate residency transition and, for U.S. citizens, potentially consider the implications of expatriation (including the exit tax under IRC §877A).

❌ Choosing a program based on cost alone. The cheapest option isn't always the best. Factors like visa-free travel scope, treaty networks, banking access, and due diligence reputation all matter — especially if your acquirer's legal team is going to scrutinize your structure.

❌ Ignoring U.S.-specific complexity. U.S. citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live. A second citizenship is still valuable for optionality, but the full tax benefit may require renunciation of U.S. citizenship — a serious, irreversible decision that demands expert counsel.

The Bottom Line

You wouldn't go into an acquisition without a clean data room, a strong legal team, and a well-structured cap table. Your personal tax and citizenship strategy deserves the same rigor.

In 2026, the founders who are winning the exit game aren't just building great products — they're building great personal structures. Second citizenship is one of the most powerful, legal, and underutilized tools available.

The best time to start was two years ago. The second best time is today.
