Why Family Offices Are Adding Second Citizenships to Their Wealth Planning Toolkit

November 17, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

The smartest money in the room is quietly diversifying beyond assets — and into passports.

In 2026, the conversation inside family offices has shifted. It's no longer enough to diversify across asset classes, geographies, and currencies. The most forward-thinking wealth advisors are now asking a question that would have seemed unusual a decade ago:

"What passports does the family hold — and is that enough?"

Citizenship by Investment (CBI) is no longer a niche product marketed to restless expats or tax-averse crypto traders. It has entered the mainstream of institutional-grade wealth planning, and family offices are leading the charge. Here's why.

1. Geopolitical Risk Is No Longer Theoretical

The past several years have delivered a masterclass in political volatility. From banking crises and capital controls to sudden visa restrictions and asset freezes, high-net-worth families have watched in real time as national borders became financial bottlenecks.

Family offices exist to protect generational wealth across decades — sometimes centuries. That mandate now explicitly includes jurisdictional diversification. A second (or third) citizenship acts as a geopolitical hedge, ensuring that no single government's decisions can lock a family out of their wealth, their mobility, or their options.

As one London-based family office principal recently put it: "We wouldn't hold 100% of our portfolio in a single stock. Why would we hold 100% of our mobility in a single passport?"

2. Tax Planning Demands Structural Flexibility

Tax optimization has always been central to family office strategy. But in 2026, the global tax landscape is more complex than ever. The OECD's global minimum tax framework, expanding CRS (Common Reporting Standard) participation, and shifting residency-based tax rules have made it critical for families to think carefully about where they are domiciled — not just where their assets are held.

A second citizenship can unlock:

  • Tax-neutral residency options in jurisdictions with no income tax, no capital gains tax, or territorial tax systems
  • Treaty access that reduces withholding on dividends, interest, and royalties
  • Estate planning flexibility, particularly for families with cross-border holdings
  • Structural optionality for trusts, holding companies, and foundations

Programs like St. Kitts & Nevis and Grenada offer citizenship in countries with zero personal income tax, while Portugal's Golden Visa provides a pathway into the EU's tax treaty network. Malta, as an EU member state, opens the door to the entire European regulatory framework.

The key insight family offices have landed on: citizenship isn't just a travel document. It's a structural planning tool.

3. The Next Generation Demands Global Mobility

One of the most underreported drivers of CBI adoption among family offices is generational. The principals may be rooted in one country, but their children and grandchildren are not.

The next generation is globally educated, globally networked, and globally ambitious. They want the freedom to:

  • Study at universities in the EU, UK, or North America without restrictive visa processes
  • Launch businesses in emerging markets
  • Live and work across borders without bureaucratic friction
  • Access healthcare systems in multiple jurisdictions

A second citizenship — especially one that grants visa-free access to 140+ countries — becomes a gift of optionality to future generations. Family offices increasingly view this not as a luxury but as an infrastructure investment in the family's long-term human capital.

4. Crypto and Digital Wealth Require Friendly Jurisdictions

The explosion of digital asset wealth has created a new class of ultra-high-net-worth individuals — many of whom are poorly served by their home country's regulatory environment.

Family offices managing portfolios with significant crypto, DeFi, or Web3 exposure are actively seeking jurisdictions that offer:

  • Clear, favorable regulatory frameworks for digital assets
  • No capital gains tax on crypto dispositions
  • Banking infrastructure that welcomes digital asset holders
  • Privacy protections consistent with global compliance standards

Caribbean CBI nations, in particular, have emerged as attractive options. St. Kitts & Nevis and Grenada offer citizenship with no tax on worldwide income, capital gains, or inheritance — a compelling combination for families whose wealth is denominated in Bitcoin, Ethereum, or tokenized assets.

5. Processing Speed and Certainty Matter

Family offices don't just care about what they're buying — they care about how fast and how reliably they can execute. This is where modern CBI programs have matured significantly.

In 2026, the leading programs offer:

| Program | Typical Timeline | Minimum Investment | Key Benefit |

|---|---|---|---|

| St. Kitts & Nevis | 60–90 days | $250,000 (donation) | Oldest, most established CBI program |

| Grenada | 90–120 days | $235,000 (donation) | Includes E-2 treaty access to the US |

| Portugal Golden Visa | 12–18 months | €500,000 (fund investment) | EU residency → citizenship pathway |

| Malta | 12–36 months | €690,000+ (combined) | Full EU citizenship |

For family offices, the ability to execute within a known timeframe — with clear legal frameworks and established due diligence processes — makes CBI a plannable, auditable strategy rather than a speculative one.

6. It's About Optionality, Not Escape

Perhaps the most important reframe happening inside family offices is this: second citizenship is not about leaving. It's about having the option to.

The wealthiest families in the world don't make decisions under pressure. They make decisions from positions of strength, with multiple paths available to them at any given moment. A second citizenship is the ultimate expression of that philosophy.

It's not a panic move. It's a chess move.

The Bottom Line

Family offices are in the business of protecting and growing wealth across generations. In a world defined by geopolitical uncertainty, regulatory complexity, and an increasingly mobile next generation, second citizenship has earned its place alongside trusts, holding structures, and diversified portfolios as a core pillar of multigenerational wealth strategy.

The question is no longer "Should we consider this?" — it's "Why haven't we done it yet?"
