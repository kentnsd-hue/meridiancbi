Why Grenada's CBI Program Is the Ultimate Gateway to the U.S. for Global Entrepreneurs

October 17, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The One CBI Program That Opens the Door to America

Every year, thousands of entrepreneurs around the world face the same frustrating paradox: the United States remains the world's largest consumer market, its most dynamic startup ecosystem, and its deepest pool of venture capital — yet getting a visa to actually live and work there is notoriously difficult.

H-1B lotteries are brutally competitive. EB-5 investor visas demand a minimum of $800,000 in a Targeted Employment Area (and often over $1 million), come with years-long processing times, and carry significant regulatory complexity. For many global founders, the front door to America feels permanently locked.

But there's a side door. And it runs through a 133-square-mile Caribbean island.

Grenada's Citizenship by Investment program is the only CBI program in the world that comes bundled with access to the U.S. E-2 Treaty Investor Visa — and in 2026, that single distinction makes it arguably the most strategically valuable second passport on the market.

What Is the E-2 Treaty Investor Visa?

The E-2 is a non-immigrant visa available to nationals of countries that maintain a Treaty of Commerce and Navigation with the United States. It allows entrepreneurs to:

  • Live and work in the U.S. by investing in and directing a real, operating business
  • Bring their spouse and dependent children (spouses receive work authorization)
  • Renew indefinitely in two-year increments, as long as the business remains active
  • Enter and exit the U.S. freely without the restrictions of tourist visas

The minimum investment threshold is not fixed by statute, but in practice, investments typically range from $100,000 to $200,000+ depending on the business type — a fraction of what an EB-5 requires.

Why Can't Everyone Access the E-2?

Here's the catch: the E-2 is only available to citizens of treaty countries. Citizens of major economic powerhouses like China, India, Brazil, the UAE, and South Africa are not eligible. Their countries simply don't have the required treaty with the United States.

This is where Grenada changes the entire equation.

Grenada: The Only CBI Passport With E-2 Access

Grenada signed a bilateral investment treaty with the United States that qualifies its citizens for E-2 status. No other active Caribbean CBI program offers this benefit:

| CBI Program | E-2 Treaty Access? |

|---|---|

| Grenada | ✅ Yes |

| St. Kitts & Nevis | ❌ No |

| Dominica | ❌ No |

| Antigua & Barbuda | ❌ No |

| St. Lucia | ❌ No |

This means that a Chinese tech founder, an Indian SaaS entrepreneur, a Nigerian fintech CEO, or a Brazilian e-commerce operator can:

1. Obtain Grenadian citizenship through a qualifying investment

2. Apply for a U.S. E-2 visa as a Grenadian national

3. Move to the United States to launch or expand a business

All without entering the H-1B lottery, without committing $800K+ to an EB-5, and without waiting years for processing.

The Numbers: Grenada CBI Investment Requirements in 2026

Grenada offers two primary investment routes:

Option 1: National Transformation Fund (NTF) Donation

  • $235,000 for a single applicant
  • $270,000 for a family of up to four
  • Non-refundable contribution to Grenada's national development fund
  • Fastest and most straightforward route

Option 2: Approved Real Estate Investment

  • Minimum $270,000 in a government-approved real estate project
  • Must be held for a minimum of 5 years
  • Potential for rental income and capital appreciation
  • Resalable after the holding period (though resale value is market-dependent)

Processing time: Approximately 4–6 months from application to passport issuance.

Note: Government fees, due diligence charges, and professional service costs are additional. All figures are subject to change based on Grenada's CBI program updates.

The Strategic Math: Grenada CBI + E-2 vs. EB-5

Let's lay out the comparison that matters most to U.S.-bound entrepreneurs:

| Factor | Grenada CBI + E-2 Route | EB-5 Investor Visa |

|---|---|---|

| Total Investment | ~$235K (NTF) + $100K–$200K (E-2 business) | $800K–$1.05M |

| Processing Time | 4–6 months (CBI) + 2–4 months (E-2) | 2–5+ years |

| Path to U.S. Residency | Live & work via E-2 (renewable indefinitely) | Green card (permanent residency) |

| Path to U.S. Citizenship | Not directly — E-2 is non-immigrant | Yes, after 5 years of residency |

| Business Control | You own and operate your business | Investment in approved project (often passive) |

| Spouse Work Authorization | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |

| Second Passport Included | ✅ Grenadian passport (visa-free to 145+ countries) | ❌ No second passport |

| Available to Chinese/Indian Nationals | ✅ Yes (through Grenadian citizenship) | ✅ Yes (but with extreme backlogs) |

For entrepreneurs who want speed, control, and flexibility — and who don't necessarily need a green card on day one — the Grenada + E-2 pathway is dramatically more efficient.

Beyond the E-2: The Full Value of a Grenadian Passport

While the E-2 treaty access is the headline benefit, Grenada's passport carries substantial standalone value:

🌍 Global Mobility

Visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 145+ countries and territories, including:

  • The entire European Union / Schengen Zone
  • The United Kingdom
  • China (30-day visa-free as of recent bilateral agreements)
  • Singapore, Hong Kong, and most of the Caribbean

🏦 Tax Optimization

Grenada has no income tax on worldwide income, no capital gains tax, no wealth tax, and no inheritance tax for residents. For digital entrepreneurs, remote founders, and investors with globally distributed income, this is a significant planning advantage.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Inclusion

A single application can include:

  • Spouse
  • Dependent children (up to age 30 if in full-time education or financially dependent)
  • Dependent parents and grandparents (age 55+)
  • Unmarried siblings of the main applicant (under certain conditions)

🔐 Plan B & Political Diversification

In an era of geopolitical uncertainty, currency controls, and shifting regulatory landscapes, a second citizenship provides an insurance policy — a Plan B that no amount of wealth alone can guarantee.

Common Questions We Hear

"Can I eventually get a U.S. green card through the E-2?"

The E-2 visa itself does not directly lead to a green card. However, it allows you to live and work in the U.S. legally while you explore other immigration pathways — such as an EB-1A (extraordinary ability), EB-2 NIW (national interest waiver), or employer sponsorship. Many entrepreneurs use the E-2 as a bridge strategy while building their U.S. business to a scale that qualifies for a more permanent route.

"Do I need to renounce my original citizenship?"

No. Grenada allows dual citizenship. You retain your original passport and gain a Grenadian passport in addition.

"How legitimate is this pathway? Will the U.S. consulate scrutinize my application?"

The E-2 visa is a well-established, fully legal visa category. U.S. consulates are accustomed to processing E-2 applications from Grenadian CBI citizens. The key is ensuring your E-2 business plan is genuine, well-capitalized, and demonstrates that the enterprise is real and not marginal. This is where professional advisory matters — both for the CBI application and the subsequent E-2 filing.

"What kind of business do I need for the E-2?"

The business must be a real, active commercial enterprise. Common examples include:

  • Franchise operations (restaurants, fitness studios, service businesses)
  • Tech companies with U.S.-based operations
  • Import/export businesses
  • Consulting or professional services firms
  • E-commerce fulfillment operations

The business cannot be "marginal" — it must have the capacity to generate income beyond just supporting you and your family.

The 2026 Landscape: Why Timing Matters

Several macro trends are making the Grenada pathway more relevant than ever this year:

1. EB-5 backlogs continue to grow. For applicants born in India and China, wait times remain measured in years, not months. The Grenada + E-2 route offers a parallel path that can be operational within 6–10 months.

2. CBI program scrutiny is tightening globally. The EU has pressured several Caribbean nations, and some programs have faced suspension or increased due diligence requirements. Grenada's program remains well-regulated and operational, but there is no guarantee that terms won't change. Locking in under current conditions is prudent.

3. Global tax reform is accelerating. OECD global minimum tax rules, CRS reporting, and increased information sharing between jurisdictions mean that tax planning requires more sophisticated structuring. A Grenadian passport is one component of a broader strategy — not a silver bullet — and the earlier it's in place, the more planning flexibility it provides.

4. Geopolitical instability is driving Plan B demand. From currency devaluations to capital controls to political upheaval, 2026 has reinforced what seasoned global citizens already know: diversification isn't just for portfolios. It's for passports.
