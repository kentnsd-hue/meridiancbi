Second Citizenship for Your Whole Family: Which CBI Programs Let You Include Parents, Siblings, and Beyond?

November 28, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

When high-net-worth individuals explore citizenship by investment, the first question is rarely about themselves. It's about their family.

"Can I include my parents?"

"What about my adult children?"

"Does my sibling qualify?"

These are the questions we hear every single week at Meridian Advisory. And the answers vary dramatically depending on which program you choose.

The reality is that not all CBI programs are created equal when it comes to family inclusion. Some are remarkably generous—extending eligibility to parents, grandparents, siblings, and adult children. Others draw a tight circle around the nuclear family and stop there.

If you're investing six or even seven figures into a second citizenship, you want to make sure the people who matter most are covered. This guide breaks down exactly which programs allow what, so you can make an informed decision for your entire family.

Why Family Inclusion Matters More Than You Think

Before we dive into the specifics, let's address the strategic importance of family-inclusive programs.

Legacy planning. A second citizenship isn't just a travel document—it's a generational asset. Including aging parents gives them access to better healthcare, visa-free travel, and a safe harbor. Including adult children sets them up with global mobility for decades to come.

Cost efficiency. Adding dependents to a single application is almost always cheaper than filing separate applications later. In many programs, the marginal cost of adding a parent or sibling is a fraction of what a standalone application would cost.

Speed and simplicity. Processing an entire family under one application means one due diligence cycle, one timeline, and one point of contact. It's cleaner, faster, and far less stressful.

Program-by-Program Family Inclusion Breakdown

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis — The Gold Standard for Family Inclusion

Established: 1984 (the world's oldest CBI program)

Minimum Investment: $250,000 (Sustainable Island State Contribution — SISC)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 31 (if financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents aged 55+ (if financially dependent)
  • ✅ Siblings under 31 (if unmarried and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Grandparents aged 55+

Why it stands out:

St. Kitts & Nevis is one of the very few programs in the world that explicitly allows siblings to be included as dependents. For families with younger unmarried brothers or sisters, this is a game-changer.

The program also has one of the most generous age thresholds for children—up to age 30—which means your university-age or even early-career adult children can be included without the pressure of rushing an application before an 18th or 25th birthday.

Cost consideration: The SISC contribution increases with each additional dependent, but the incremental cost is significantly lower than sponsoring a separate application. A family of four typically invests around $250,000–$300,000 through the contribution route.

🇬🇩 Grenada — Family-Friendly with a Powerful Bonus

Minimum Investment: $235,000 (National Transformation Fund) or $270,000 (real estate)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30 (if enrolled in higher education and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents aged 55+ (of either main applicant or spouse)
  • ✅ Siblings of the main applicant (unmarried, childless, under 18)

Why it stands out:

Grenada's real differentiator isn't just family inclusion—it's the E-2 Treaty Investor Visa agreement with the United States. Grenada is the only Caribbean CBI nation that holds this treaty, meaning your entire family can use Grenadian citizenship as a pathway to live and work in the U.S.

For families with parents who want to spend time near children studying or working in America, this is an extraordinarily valuable feature.

The sibling inclusion in Grenada is more restrictive than St. Kitts (under 18 vs. under 31), so it's primarily useful for applicants with minor siblings or for families with complex guardianship situations.

Cost consideration: Additional dependents through the NTF route add approximately $25,000–$50,000 per person depending on age and relationship, making it one of the more cost-effective family programs.

🇦🇬 Antigua & Barbuda — Built for Larger Families

Minimum Investment: $230,000 (National Development Fund) or $300,000 (real estate)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30
  • ✅ Parents aged 55+ (of main applicant or spouse)
  • ✅ Siblings under 18 (unmarried and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Grandparents aged 55+

Why it stands out:

Antigua & Barbuda offers one of the most competitively priced family options in the Caribbean. Their NDF contribution has a family pricing structure that caps costs for families of four or more, making it particularly attractive for larger households.

Additionally, Antigua requires at least five days of residency within the first five years—one of the lightest physical presence requirements of any CBI program, which is ideal for busy families who need flexibility.

The inclusion of grandparents on both sides of the family is a meaningful differentiator for multigenerational families.

🇩🇲 Dominica — Affordable but More Limited

Minimum Investment: $200,000 (Economic Diversification Fund)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 30 (if financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents aged 65+ (of main applicant or spouse)
  • ✅ Siblings under 18 (unmarried, childless, financially dependent)

Why it stands out:

Dominica is consistently ranked as one of the most affordable CBI programs in the world, and it has expanded its dependent eligibility in recent years. However, it's worth noting that the parent age threshold is 65, which is notably higher than most other Caribbean programs (which set the bar at 55).

This means if your parents are between 55 and 64, Dominica may not be the right fit unless they qualify under other dependency criteria.

Cost consideration: Dominica's pricing for a single applicant starts at $200,000, with family applications scaling modestly. It's often the entry point for families who are cost-conscious but still want Caribbean citizenship with strong visa-free travel (140+ countries).

🇲🇹 Malta — The European Powerhouse

Minimum Investment: ~€690,000–€890,000+ (contribution, real estate, and philanthropic donation combined)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse
  • ✅ Children under 29 (if unmarried and financially dependent)
  • ✅ Parents aged 55+ (if financially dependent)
  • ❌ Siblings are not included

Why it stands out:

Malta's Citizenship by Naturalisation for Exceptional Services by Direct Investment (the official name of its CBI program) grants EU citizenship, which is in a category of its own. We're talking about the right to live, work, and study anywhere in the 27 EU member states, plus visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 180+ countries.

The tradeoff is that siblings cannot be added, the investment threshold is significantly higher, and the process includes a 12-to-36-month residency requirement before citizenship is granted (depending on investment level).

For families whose priority is Europe—whether for business, education, healthcare, or retirement—Malta is unmatched.

Cost consideration: The all-in cost for a family of four typically ranges from €800,000 to over €1 million, making it the premium option. But for EU access, many families consider it well worth the investment.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa — Residency First, Citizenship Later

Minimum Investment: €500,000 (investment funds route, as real estate is no longer eligible in Lisbon/Porto)

Who can be included:

  • ✅ Spouse or partner
  • ✅ Children under 18 (or under 26 if enrolled in education)
  • ✅ Parents aged 65+ (of main applicant or spouse, if financially dependent)
  • ❌ Siblings are not included

Important distinction: Portugal's Golden Visa is a residency program, not direct citizenship. However, after five years of holding residency and meeting minimal physical presence requirements (just seven days per year), applicants can apply for Portuguese (and therefore EU) citizenship.

Why it stands out:

For families willing to take the five-year pathway, Portugal offers arguably the best value proposition for EU citizenship. The investment threshold is lower than Malta, the physical presence requirement is minimal, and Portuguese citizenship grants the same EU-wide mobility rights.

The parent inclusion requires a higher age threshold (65 vs. 55 in many Caribbean programs), and siblings are excluded. But for nuclear families with elderly parents, it's a powerful long-term play.

So Which Program Is Right for Your Family?

The answer depends on three key factors:

1. Who do you need to include?

If siblings are a priority, St. Kitts & Nevis is the clear frontrunner. If you're focused on parents and children only, you have more flexibility across all programs.

2. What's your geographic priority?

If EU access is non-negotiable, your options narrow to Malta (faster, more expensive) or Portugal (slower, more affordable). If visa-free global travel and speed are the priority, the Caribbean programs deliver in 3–6 months.

3. What's your budget?

For families seeking the most cost-effective route with broad dependent eligibility, Dominica and Antigua & Barbuda offer compelling value. For those who view citizenship as a cornerstone investment, Malta delivers unparalleled returns in terms of global access.

A Note on "Financially Dependent"

You'll notice the phrase "financially dependent" appears throughout this guide. Every CBI program requires that included dependents demonstrate financial dependency on the main applicant. This typically means:

  • The dependent does not have independent means of significant income
  • The main applicant provides material financial support
  • Documentation (bank statements, affidavits, tax records) supports the claim

This is not merely a checkbox. Due diligence teams scrutinize dependent claims carefully. Working with an experienced advisory firm ensures your documentation is bulletproof and your family's application moves forward without delays.

The Bottom Line

Second citizenship is one of the most significant investments a family can make—not just financially, but strategically. The right program protects your parents' retirement years, opens doors for your children's futures, and in some cases, keeps your siblings within the same circle of global mobility.

But the details matter enormously. Age cutoffs, dependency requirements, additional government fees per dependent, and processing timelines all vary in ways that can cost you tens of thousands of dollars or months of delays if you choose the wrong path.
