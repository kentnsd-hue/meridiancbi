Why Grenada's CBI Program Is the Ultimate Gateway to the US Market for Entrepreneurs

September 07, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

The only Caribbean citizenship-by-investment that unlocks America's E-2 visa — and why savvy founders are paying attention in 2026.

The United States remains the world's largest consumer market, the deepest talent pool, and the most dynamic startup ecosystem on the planet. For international entrepreneurs, getting in — legally, efficiently, and with operational flexibility — is the hard part.

Most founders hit the same wall: the US has no straightforward "entrepreneur visa." The EB-5 demands $800,000+ in capital and years of processing. The H-1B is a lottery designed for employees, not founders. The O-1A is reserved for those who can prove extraordinary ability with extensive documentation.

Then there's the E-2 Treaty Investor Visa — a powerful, renewable, non-immigrant visa that lets entrepreneurs live and work in the US by investing in a real business. The catch? It's only available to citizens of countries that hold an E-2 treaty with the United States.

And this is exactly where Grenada enters the conversation.

What Is the E-2 Visa, and Why Does It Matter?

The E-2 Treaty Investor Visa allows citizens of treaty nations to:

  • Live and work in the US by directing and developing a business they've invested in
  • Bring their spouse and dependent children (spouses receive open work authorization)
  • Renew indefinitely in two-year increments, as long as the business remains operational
  • Enter and exit the US freely, maintaining global mobility
  • Start operating in months, not years — processing times typically range from 2–6 months

Unlike the EB-5, the E-2 doesn't require creating 10 full-time jobs for US workers. Unlike the H-1B, there's no annual cap or lottery. Unlike the L-1, you don't need an existing foreign company with a qualifying relationship.

The E-2 is, in practice, the closest thing the US immigration system offers to a founder-friendly visa.

The minimum investment isn't fixed by statute, but successful applications typically involve $100,000–$200,000+ deployed into a legitimate, active US business — a threshold well within reach for most high-net-worth entrepreneurs.

The Grenada Advantage: Where CBI Meets US Market Access

Grenada is the only country in the Caribbean — and one of very few globally — that offers both a citizenship-by-investment program and an E-2 treaty with the United States.

Read that again. It's the linchpin of the entire strategy.

Here's how it works:

1. Acquire Grenadian citizenship through the country's CBI program

2. Apply for the E-2 visa as a Grenadian national

3. Launch or invest in a US-based business and relocate with your family

No other Caribbean CBI program — not St. Kitts, not Dominica, not Antigua — can offer this pathway. Their citizens are simply not eligible for the E-2.

Grenada's CBI Program: The Numbers in 2026

Grenada's Citizenship by Investment Programme offers two routes:

Option 1: National Transformation Fund (NTF) Donation

| Applicant Type | Contribution |

|---|---|

| Single applicant | $235,000 |

| Applicant + spouse | $270,000 |

| Family of four | $290,000 |

| Additional dependents | $25,000 each (over 18) / $50,000 (siblings) |

Plus government processing and due diligence fees.

Option 2: Approved Real Estate Investment

  • Minimum investment: $270,000 (for approved shared ownership projects) or $350,000 for sole ownership properties
  • Must be held for a minimum of 5 years
  • Option to resell after the holding period, potentially recovering a significant portion of the investment

Processing time: Approximately 4–6 months from application to citizenship approval.

Due diligence: Grenada maintains rigorous vetting standards through its Citizenship by Investment Committee (CBIC), which has helped preserve the program's international credibility — a critical factor when your end goal involves a US visa application.

Why This Strategy Is Gaining Momentum in 2026

Several converging trends are driving record interest in the Grenada-to-E-2 pipeline:

1. US Immigration Backlogs Are Worsening

EB-5 processing times have ballooned. For applicants from high-demand countries like India and China, wait times can stretch 10–15+ years due to per-country visa caps. The E-2, by contrast, is processed at US consulates abroad with no annual cap and no per-country quota.

2. Founders Want Optionality, Not Permanence (Yet)

The E-2 is a non-immigrant visa — it doesn't directly lead to a green card. But for many entrepreneurs, that's actually a feature. It allows you to operate in the US, test a market, build revenue, and decide later whether to pursue permanent residency through an EB-1A, EB-2 NIW, or EB-5 — from a position of strength, with a running US business.

3. Geopolitical Hedging Is Now Standard Practice

In an era of rising economic nationalism, shifting tax regimes, and unpredictable policy changes, second citizenship isn't a luxury — it's a risk management tool. Grenadian citizenship offers visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 140+ countries, including the UK, EU Schengen zone, China, Singapore, and more.

4. The Crypto and Tech Wealth Wave

A new generation of founders and investors — many of whom built wealth in decentralized finance, Web3, AI, and SaaS — are seeking jurisdictions that offer flexibility, favorable tax treatment, and global access. Grenada checks every box.

The Full Picture: What a Grenadian Passport Actually Gets You

Beyond E-2 eligibility, Grenadian citizenship delivers:

  • Visa-free travel to 140+ destinations, including the UK (6 months), EU/Schengen (90 days), China, Singapore, Hong Kong, and more
  • No personal income tax, no capital gains tax, no wealth tax, and no inheritance tax in Grenada
  • Dual citizenship permitted — Grenada places no restrictions, and your current nationality is unaffected
  • Inclusion of dependents — spouse, children, parents, grandparents, and even unmarried siblings can be included on a single application
  • No residency requirement — you never need to live in or even visit Grenada (though you might want to — it's stunning)

  • Digital nomads and remote founders who've outgrown tourist visas and need a legitimate operational base in the US

Common Questions We Hear

"Can I go straight from Grenadian citizenship to an E-2 approval?"

Yes. Once you hold a Grenadian passport, you're eligible to apply for the E-2 at a US consulate. There's no minimum waiting period between obtaining citizenship and filing your E-2 application — though having a well-structured business plan and substantive investment is critical to approval.

"How much do I need to invest in the US business?"

There's no statutory minimum, but in practice, most successful E-2 applications involve $100,000–$200,000+ in a real, active, at-risk business. The investment must be "substantial" relative to the total cost of the enterprise, and you must demonstrate that the business is not marginal — meaning it has the capacity to generate income beyond just supporting you and your family.

"Is the E-2 renewable forever?"

In theory, yes. The E-2 can be renewed in two-year increments indefinitely, as long as the underlying business continues to operate and you maintain your treaty-nation citizenship. Many E-2 holders have lived in the US for decades on this visa.

"Does my family get to come?"

Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 are eligible for derivative E-2 status. Your spouse will receive an Employment Authorization Document (EAD), allowing them to work for any US employer — a major benefit that most other visa categories don't offer.

The Bottom Line

If you're an entrepreneur with global ambitions and the US market in your sights, Grenada's CBI program isn't just a good option — it's arguably the single most strategically valuable citizenship-by-investment available in 2026.

No other program combines:

✅ A fast, well-regulated path to citizenship (4–6 months)

✅ Direct eligibility for the US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa

✅ Visa-free access to 140+ countries

✅ Zero income, capital gains, or wealth tax

✅ Full family inclusion with no residency requirements

It's a rare convergence of benefits — and for the right founder, it's a game-changer.
