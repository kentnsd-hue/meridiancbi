The Future of Citizenship by Investment: Trends & Predictions for 2027

November 01, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

The citizenship by investment (CBI) landscape has never been more dynamic — or more consequential. As we move through 2026, the forces reshaping global mobility are accelerating: tightening EU regulations, surging demand from tech entrepreneurs and crypto investors, and a fundamental rethinking of what citizenship even means in an increasingly borderless economy.

At Meridian Advisory, we spend every day tracking these shifts on behalf of our clients. Here's where we see the CBI industry heading in 2027 — and what it means for high-net-worth individuals considering their options right now.

1. The EU Will Continue Tightening — But Won't Shut the Door Entirely

The European Commission's sustained pressure on "golden passport" programs has already reshaped the market. Following increased scrutiny in 2025 and 2026, we expect 2027 to bring:

  • Stricter due diligence standards adopted as a baseline across all EU-adjacent programs, particularly in Malta and Portugal.
  • Longer processing timelines as member states implement enhanced vetting protocols to satisfy Brussels.
  • Higher minimum investment thresholds — Malta has already signaled potential adjustments, and Portugal's Golden Visa restructuring continues to evolve.

What this means for you: The window for accessing European residency and citizenship programs at current investment levels is narrowing. Applicants who begin the process in 2026 will almost certainly benefit from more favorable terms than those who wait until 2027 or beyond.

2. Caribbean Programs Will Solidify Their Position as the Gold Standard for Speed and Value

While Europe tightens, the Caribbean continues to deliver what high-net-worth individuals value most: efficiency, reliability, and genuine global mobility.

By 2027, we predict:

  • St. Kitts & Nevis will further cement its reputation as the "Platinum Standard" of CBI, potentially introducing tiered investment options to attract a broader range of qualified applicants while maintaining its rigorous vetting.
  • Grenada will see increased demand, largely driven by its unique E-2 Treaty with the United States — still the only Caribbean CBI nation offering a direct pathway to living and working in the U.S.
  • Regional harmonization among Caribbean CBI nations will deepen, with coordinated minimum pricing, shared due diligence databases, and unified marketing efforts.

The bottom line: Caribbean programs aren't a "Plan B." For many of our clients — particularly founders, crypto investors, and digital nomads — they're the optimal first move.

3. Crypto and Digital Asset Holders Will Become the Fastest-Growing Client Segment

This trend has been building for years, but 2027 will be the inflection point. Here's why:

  • Regulatory uncertainty around digital assets in major economies (the U.S., EU, and parts of Asia) is pushing crypto-wealthy individuals to seek jurisdictions with clearer, more favorable frameworks.
  • Tax optimization remains a primary driver. Second citizenship in jurisdictions with no capital gains tax on crypto can represent millions in savings.
  • Several CBI programs are now accepting cryptocurrency for investment contributions, removing a significant friction point.

We're already seeing a surge in inquiries from blockchain founders, DeFi investors, and NFT creators in 2026. By 2027, we expect this segment to represent 25–30% of all new CBI applications globally.

4. "Citizenship Planning" Will Replace "Citizenship Shopping"

The era of choosing a passport based solely on cost or processing time is ending. In 2027, we expect a decisive shift toward strategic citizenship planning — a holistic approach that considers:

  • Tax residency implications across multiple jurisdictions
  • Business expansion goals and bilateral trade agreements
  • Family legacy planning, including generational wealth transfer and education access
  • Geopolitical risk hedging, particularly for individuals in politically volatile regions
  • Healthcare and quality-of-life benchmarks

This is the approach Meridian Advisory has always advocated. A second passport isn't a product — it's an element of a comprehensive global strategy.

5. Demand From U.S. Citizens and Residents Will Hit Record Highs

Regardless of political affiliation, American entrepreneurs and investors are increasingly recognizing the value of optionality. In 2026, we've already seen a marked increase in U.S.-based inquiries driven by:

  • Concerns about tax policy direction and potential capital gains increases
  • A desire for geographic diversification of both personal residence and business operations
  • Growing awareness that a second citizenship is a mainstream wealth management tool, not an extreme measure

By 2027, we predict U.S. citizens will represent the single largest nationality among new CBI applicants worldwide.

6. Due Diligence Will Become a Competitive Advantage, Not a Burden

In the early days of CBI, rigorous vetting was seen as an obstacle. That narrative has flipped entirely.

In 2027, the programs that thrive will be those with the most credible, transparent, and internationally respected due diligence processes. Why?

  • Passport strength correlates directly with program integrity. Visa-free access is maintained through trust, and trust is built through vetting.
  • Applicants themselves prefer robust screening. Our clients don't want to hold a passport associated with lax standards. They want a credential that commands respect at every border crossing.
  • International bodies like the OECD, FATF, and EU will increasingly reward programs that exceed minimum standards.

This is exactly why we guide the majority of our clients toward St. Kitts & Nevis and Grenada — programs where the due diligence process enhances the value of the passport rather than diminishing it.

7. New Entrants and Program Innovations Will Emerge

The CBI market is not static. By 2027, watch for:

  • New programs in Southeast Asia and the Pacific targeting digital nomads and remote workers with hybrid residency/citizenship models.
  • Real estate tokenization as an accepted investment vehicle within existing CBI programs, allowing fractional property ownership to qualify.
  • Sustainability-linked investment options, where contributions fund renewable energy, climate resilience, or marine conservation — particularly in Caribbean nations vulnerable to climate change.
  • Faster processing through AI-driven due diligence, potentially reducing approval timelines from months to weeks for straightforward applications.

What Should You Do Right Now?

If you've been considering a second citizenship, the data points in one direction: the optimal time to act is before the market shifts further.

Investment thresholds are rising. Processing standards are tightening. And the programs that offer the best combination of value, mobility, and prestige today may look very different by the end of 2027.

Here's what we recommend:

1. Assess your goals. Is this about tax optimization? Global mobility? Family security? Business expansion? The answer shapes everything.

2. Understand your timeline. Some programs deliver citizenship in 60 days. Others take 12–18 months. Start with the end in mind.

3. Work with advisors who think strategically, not transactionally. This is a decision that will impact your family for generations.
