The Future of Citizenship by Investment: Trends & Predictions for 2027

October 09, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | January 2026

The citizenship by investment (CBI) landscape has never been more dynamic. As we move through 2026, the forces reshaping global mobility — geopolitical instability, digital wealth creation, tightening regulatory frameworks, and the rise of a truly borderless professional class — are accelerating at a pace that demands attention.

At Meridian Advisory, we advise clients who think years ahead, not months. So where is the CBI industry heading as we look toward 2027? Here are the trends and predictions we believe every globally minded investor should have on their radar.

1. Due Diligence Standards Will Reach an All-Time High

The era of "fast and loose" CBI processing is definitively over.

Throughout 2025 and into 2026, we've watched Caribbean nations, EU member states, and international oversight bodies like the OECD and the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) tighten vetting procedures significantly. By 2027, expect:

  • Multi-layered biometric screening becoming standard across all reputable programs
  • Source-of-funds verification that rivals Tier 1 banking onboarding processes
  • Cross-jurisdictional data sharing between CBI units and international intelligence databases
  • Ongoing monitoring of approved citizens, not just at the application stage

What this means for you: Programs with the strongest due diligence will become the most valuable. A passport from a jurisdiction known for rigorous vetting carries more weight — more visa-free destinations, more banking access, more reputational credibility. Cutting corners today creates liability tomorrow.

2. The EU Will Further Restrict — But Not Eliminate — Golden Visa Pathways

The European Commission has been pressuring member states to wind down residency-and-citizenship-for-investment schemes for years. Portugal's Golden Visa underwent major revisions. Cyprus shut its program down entirely. Malta has faced repeated scrutiny.

By 2027, we predict:

  • Portugal will continue to offer a modified Golden Visa, likely with higher investment thresholds and stricter residency requirements — but it won't disappear entirely. The economic incentive is too significant.
  • Malta will remain the gold standard for EU citizenship by investment, but processing times will lengthen and the annual cap on approvals may tighten further.
  • Greece and Spain may introduce new restrictions on real estate-based residency routes, following the broader EU trend.
  • A new Eastern European or Balkan pathway could emerge as candidate countries seek foreign investment to bolster EU accession bids.

What this means for you: The window for EU-linked investment migration is narrowing, not widening. Clients who act in 2026 will secure terms that may not exist by 2028.

3. Caribbean Programs Will Consolidate and Professionalize

The five Caribbean CBI nations — St. Kitts & Nevis, Dominica, Grenada, Antigua & Barbuda, and St. Lucia — have been moving toward harmonized standards since the 2023 Memorandum of Agreement. By 2027, we expect this consolidation to deepen:

  • Minimum investment thresholds will likely stabilize or increase modestly across all five programs
  • A shared compliance framework could emerge, creating a unified Caribbean CBI standard
  • St. Kitts & Nevis will continue to command a premium as the oldest and most established program, with its recent reforms reinforcing its credibility
  • Grenada will maintain its unique advantage as the only Caribbean CBI nation with access to the US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa — a feature we expect to become even more valuable as US immigration policy tightens

What this means for you: The Caribbean remains the fastest and most cost-effective route to a second citizenship. But the programs are maturing. The "race to the bottom" on pricing is over; the race to the top on quality has begun.

4. Crypto and Digital Asset Holders Will Become the Dominant CBI Client Segment

This is a shift we're already seeing in 2026, and it will only intensify.

The crypto economy has created a generation of wealthy individuals who are:

  • Geographically untethered — they can work from anywhere
  • Acutely aware of jurisdictional risk — they've watched regulatory crackdowns in real time
  • Motivated by tax optimization — capital gains treatment varies enormously by country
  • Younger than traditional CBI clients — many are in their 30s and 40s

By 2027, expect CBI programs to:

  • Develop clearer policies on accepting crypto-sourced wealth for investment and due diligence purposes
  • Partner with blockchain analytics firms to verify source of funds from digital assets
  • Market more aggressively to the Web3 and DeFi community

What this means for you: If your wealth is partially or fully in digital assets, securing a second citizenship isn't a luxury — it's a risk management strategy. The jurisdictions that get crypto-friendly policies right first will attract the most sophisticated applicants.

5. "Plan B" Will Become Mainstream, Not Niche

Five years ago, the concept of a "Plan B" citizenship was associated with doomsday preppers or ultra-wealthy tax exiles. That perception has fundamentally changed.

In 2026, our clients include:

  • Tech founders who want optionality in case of IPO-related tax events
  • Parents who want their children to have access to global education and healthcare
  • Retirees seeking warm-climate residency with lower cost of living
  • Professionals in unstable regions who need a genuine safety net
  • Dual-income couples building a diversified family portfolio that includes citizenship assets

By 2027, we predict that second citizenship will be discussed in the same breath as estate planning, asset protection, and international diversification — because that's exactly what it is.

What this means for you: The stigma is gone. The strategic value is clear. The only question is which program aligns with your specific goals.

6. Processing Times Will Become a Competitive Differentiator

As demand increases and compliance requirements tighten, some programs will become significantly faster than others. Speed — without sacrificing quality — will be a key selling point.

By 2027:

  • The fastest Caribbean programs will deliver citizenship in 60–90 days
  • Malta and Portugal will likely see timelines of 12–18+ months
  • Programs that invest in digital infrastructure and AI-assisted processing will pull ahead

What this means for you: If speed matters — and for many clients, it does — program selection becomes a strategic decision, not just a financial one.

7. Family-Inclusive Applications Will Expand

More programs are broadening their definitions of "dependent" to include:

  • Adult children up to age 30 (and in some cases, with no age cap if financially dependent)
  • Parents and grandparents of the main applicant
  • Siblings in select programs

By 2027, we expect this trend to continue as programs compete for multi-generational family applications, which represent higher investment amounts and stronger long-term ties to the host country.

What this means for you: A single application can secure citizenship for your entire family. The ROI per family member makes this one of the most efficient wealth-protection strategies available.

Looking Ahead: The Bottom Line

The CBI industry in 2027 will be more regulated, more professionalized, more expensive, and more valuable than it is today.

Programs will close, thresholds will rise, and the compliance bar will keep climbing. But for qualified investors who move strategically, second citizenship will remain one of the most powerful tools for protecting wealth, expanding freedom, and creating generational optionality.

The clients who will benefit the most in 2027 are the ones taking action in 2026.
