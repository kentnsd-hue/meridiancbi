Why Crypto Founders Are Getting Second Passports Before Their Next Vesting Event

October 14, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

The smartest move in crypto isn't on-chain — it's in your passport.

The 2026 crypto landscape looks radically different from even two years ago. Regulatory frameworks are tightening across the US and EU. Tax enforcement on digital assets has evolved from scattered audits to coordinated, cross-border intelligence sharing. And tokenized equity, protocol treasuries, and founder vesting schedules are creating taxable events of a magnitude that most traditional wealth advisors have never encountered.

Against this backdrop, a quiet but unmistakable trend has emerged: crypto founders are obtaining second citizenships months — sometimes years — before their next major vesting event. Not as an afterthought. As a core component of their financial architecture.

Here's why — and what they know that most people don't.

The Vesting Event Problem

If you're a protocol founder or early team member sitting on a multi-year token vesting schedule, you already understand the math. When those tokens unlock, they don't just hit your wallet. They hit your tax jurisdiction's ledger.

In the United States, token vesting events are treated as ordinary income at the fair market value on the date of receipt. At the top federal bracket of 37%, plus state taxes that can reach 13.3% in California, a $10 million vest can translate to over $5 million in combined tax liability — before you've sold a single token.

And that's assuming the regulatory environment remains static. In 2026, the IRS's expanded digital asset reporting rules under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act are fully operational. Exchanges, DeFi front-ends, and even certain wallet providers now issue 1099-DA forms. The era of ambiguity is over.

The question isn't whether the tax bill is coming. It's whether you've structured your life to manage it legally and intelligently.

Why a Second Passport — and Why Now

A second citizenship isn't a tax trick. It's a jurisdictional strategy. And for crypto founders, the window to implement it responsibly is before a liquidity event — not after.

Here's the critical nuance most people miss: changing your tax residency after a vesting event or token sale provides zero benefit for that event. Tax authorities look at where you were domiciled when the income was realized. Retroactive restructuring doesn't work. It triggers audits. And in some cases, it triggers fraud investigations.

What does work is establishing genuine residency and, where applicable, citizenship in a jurisdiction with a more favorable tax framework well in advance of realizing gains. This is not aggressive. This is not gray-area planning. This is what sovereign nations explicitly design their citizenship-by-investment programs to facilitate.

The founders who are doing this correctly are making the move 12 to 24 months before their next major vest. They're relocating genuinely, establishing ties, and building a defensible residency position that no audit can dismantle.

The Programs Crypto Founders Are Choosing in 2026

Not all CBI programs are created equal, and crypto founders have specific requirements that narrow the field considerably. They need jurisdictions that are:

  • Crypto-friendly in regulation and banking infrastructure
  • Tax-efficient on worldwide income, capital gains, or both
  • Fast to process, given that vesting schedules don't wait
  • Reputable, with strong passport mobility and no FATF blacklist concerns

Here are the programs we're seeing the most traction with at Meridian Advisory:

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis

The gold standard of CBI, and for good reason. St. Kitts imposes zero income tax, zero capital gains tax, and zero wealth tax. The program has been operational since 1984, making it the longest-running CBI program in the world. Processing can be completed in as little as 60 days under the Accelerated Application Process. For founders who need speed and certainty, St. Kitts remains the top choice.

Minimum investment: $250,000 (Sustainable Island State Contribution)

🇬🇩 Grenada

Grenada offers a unique advantage that no other Caribbean CBI program can match: access to the US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa. For crypto founders who want a tax-efficient home base and the ability to live and operate in the United States, Grenada is the only program that bridges both worlds. No income tax on foreign-sourced earnings.

Minimum investment: $235,000 (National Transformation Fund)

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa

Portugal's restructured Golden Visa program continues to attract crypto founders who want a foothold in the EU. While Portugal has updated its tax regime for Non-Habitual Residents, the country remains significantly more favorable than most Western European jurisdictions for carefully structured digital asset holdings. EU passport access after five years is the long play here.

Minimum investment: €500,000 (qualifying fund subscriptions)

🇲🇹 Malta

Malta has positioned itself as the "Blockchain Island" and backs that claim with regulatory substance. The Malta Digital Innovation Authority provides a clear legal framework for token issuers and protocol developers. Combined with a favorable tax residency regime and EU membership, Malta appeals to founders who want to stay operationally close to their projects.

The Real Risk: Doing Nothing

Let's be direct about what inaction looks like.

A founder with 2 million tokens vesting over 36 months at, say, $15 per token is looking at $30 million in realized value. Domiciled in a high-tax jurisdiction with no advance planning, they could face a combined effective tax rate north of 45%. That's $13.5 million — gone. Not reinvested in the protocol. Not allocated to the next venture. Not compounding in any form.

Compare that to a founder who obtained St. Kitts citizenship 18 months prior, established genuine residency, and relocated their center of life. The same $30 million vest is realized in a zero-income-tax jurisdiction. Legally. Defensibly. With a passport that provides visa-free access to over 155 countries.

The delta isn't marginal. It's generational.

And this isn't hypothetical. At Meridian Advisory, we've guided multiple crypto founders through exactly this sequence. The ones who act early have options. The ones who wait have tax bills.

What About Renunciation?

This is the question that comes up in nearly every consultation, particularly with US-based founders.

US citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live. A second passport alone doesn't change that. For US founders, the full benefit of jurisdictional restructuring typically requires renunciation of US citizenship — a decision that carries its own exit tax implications, reporting requirements, and deeply personal considerations.

Here's what matters strategically: you cannot renounce US citizenship without holding another citizenship first. That means the CBI process isn't step five of the plan. It's step one. Founders who begin the process early give themselves the optionality to make that decision from a position of strength, with legal counsel, proper valuations, and a clear timeline — rather than scrambling under deadline pressure.

We never advise renunciation lightly. But we always advise having the option.
