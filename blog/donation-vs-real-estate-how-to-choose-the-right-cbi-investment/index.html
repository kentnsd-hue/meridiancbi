Donation vs. Real Estate: How to Choose the Right CBI Investment Route

November 25, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

You've made the decision to pursue second citizenship through investment. Now comes the question that trips up even the most sophisticated investors: should you go the donation route or the real estate route?

It's not a simple answer — and anyone who tells you otherwise probably isn't looking at your full financial picture. The right path depends on your goals, your timeline, your liquidity, and what you actually want from your investment beyond the passport.

Let's break it down honestly.

The Two Main CBI Routes, Explained

Most Citizenship by Investment programs offer two primary pathways:

1. The Donation (Non-Refundable Contribution)

You make a one-time contribution to a government fund — typically a national development fund, a sustainability fund, or a similar sovereign initiative. The money is gone. You receive citizenship in return.

2. The Real Estate Investment

You purchase approved real estate — usually a resort, hotel share, or residential property — above a minimum threshold. You hold it for a set period (typically 3–7 years), after which you can sell. You receive citizenship upon making the qualifying investment.

Both routes lead to the same destination: full citizenship and a passport. But the journey — and what you're left with afterward — looks very different.

Donation Route: The Case For

Lower Total Outlay

In almost every program, the donation is significantly less than the real estate minimum. Here's how the numbers stack up in 2026:

| Program | Donation (Single Applicant) | Real Estate Minimum |

|---|---|---|

| St. Kitts & Nevis | From $250,000 | From $400,000 |

| Grenada | From $235,000 | From $270,000 |

| Dominica | From $200,000 | From $200,000 |

| Antigua & Barbuda | From $230,000 | From $300,000 |

Note: These figures are approximate and subject to change. Due diligence fees, government processing fees, and family-dependent costs are additional.

Faster Processing

Donation applications are typically more straightforward. There's no property due diligence, no title verification, no purchase agreement negotiation. In many programs, the donation route shaves 2–6 weeks off total processing time.

Simplicity

You wire the funds. The government confirms receipt. Your application proceeds. There is no asset to manage, no rental income to track, no property taxes in a foreign jurisdiction, no eventual resale to coordinate.

Best for: Applicants who value speed and simplicity, who want the lowest possible cash outlay, and who have no interest in owning Caribbean or European property.

Real Estate Route: The Case For

You Retain an Asset

This is the fundamental difference. With the real estate route, your capital isn't gone — it's deployed. You own something. That property may generate rental income during the holding period, and you can sell it after the mandatory hold (typically 5–7 years).

Potential Return on Investment

In well-managed resort developments and high-demand locations, CBI-approved properties can generate annual rental yields in the range of 2–5%. While this won't make you rich, it offsets a portion of your total investment cost. When you eventually sell, you may recover a meaningful percentage of your initial outlay — sometimes all of it.

Portfolio Diversification

For investors already heavy in equities, crypto, or domestic real estate, owning property in a Caribbean or European jurisdiction adds geographic diversification. It's a tangible, hard asset in a stable legal environment.

Some Programs Require It

If you're eyeing Grenada specifically for its E-2 Treaty Investor Visa pathway into the United States, or Portugal's Golden Visa for EU residency, the real estate route may offer strategic advantages that the donation simply can't replicate.

Best for: Investors who think long-term, who want to recover capital, who value portfolio diversification, or who plan to actually use the property.

The Questions You Should Actually Be Asking

Forget the brochures. Here's the real decision framework:

1. What's Your Liquidity Situation?

If tying up $400,000+ in a foreign property for five years creates financial strain, the donation route is the smarter play. CBI should enhance your financial freedom — not restrict it.

2. Do You Want a Property, or Do You Want a Passport?

Be honest with yourself. If you have zero interest in visiting, managing, or even thinking about a condo in the Caribbean, the real estate route becomes a burden disguised as an investment. The donation is cleaner.

3. What's Your Tax Situation?

Owning foreign real estate creates reporting obligations in many jurisdictions. U.S. citizens and tax residents, for example, face FBAR, FATCA, and potentially FIRPTA implications. The donation route creates none of these headaches.

4. What's Your Timeline?

Need citizenship in under 90 days? The donation route is almost always faster. If you have 6–12 months and want to be strategic, real estate becomes viable.

5. Are You Trying to Recoup Capital?

If recovering your investment matters to you, real estate is the only path. But go in with realistic expectations: CBI-approved properties are not Manhattan condos. Resale markets can be thin, and returns are modest. Talk to an advisor who will show you actual resale data — not projected yields from a developer's glossy PDF.

6. Is There a Strategic Reason for a Specific Program?

Some programs pair better with one route:

  • Grenada + Real Estate → Unlocks U.S. E-2 treaty access
  • Portugal Golden Visa + Real Estate → EU residency and path to citizenship (though fund investment options have gained traction since the 2023 changes)
  • St. Kitts + Donation → Fastest processing, most established program
  • Malta → Entirely different structure combining contribution, property, and philanthropy

Common Mistakes We See

After advising hundreds of clients at Meridian Advisory, these are the patterns we see again and again:

❌ Choosing real estate purely because "at least I get something back"

The sunk cost mindset leads people to overspend. If your primary goal is the passport, the donation is often the more economically rational choice once you factor in holding costs, management fees, taxes, and the opportunity cost of locked-up capital.

❌ Choosing donation without considering the full family picture

For larger families (4+ dependents), the per-person economics shift. In some programs, the real estate route becomes comparatively more attractive for bigger families because the property cost stays fixed while donation fees scale per dependent.

❌ Ignoring due diligence on the developer

If you go real estate, the developer matters as much as the program. We've seen clients burned by projects that stalled, never delivered rental income, or had no viable resale path. Always insist on independent property due diligence — separate from the CBI agent promoting the project.

❌ Making this decision alone

This isn't a decision you should make from a blog post — including this one. The interplay between your tax residency, family structure, business interests, travel patterns, and long-term goals is complex. It demands a personalized conversation.

The Bottom Line

There's no universally "better" route. There's only the route that's better for you — given your financial position, your family, your goals, and your appetite for complexity.

The donation is not "throwing money away." It's paying a known, fixed price for citizenship with zero residual obligations.

The real estate route is not "free citizenship." It's deploying capital into a foreign asset with moderate returns and meaningful holding requirements.

Both work. Both deliver the same passport. The difference is everything that surrounds it.
