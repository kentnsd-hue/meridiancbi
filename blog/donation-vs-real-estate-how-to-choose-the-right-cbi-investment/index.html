Donation vs. Real Estate: How to Choose the Right CBI Investment Route

August 29, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

Every year, thousands of high-net-worth individuals make the decision to pursue a second citizenship through investment. But once that decision is made, a second — equally important — question follows almost immediately:

Should I go with the donation route or the real estate investment route?

It's a question we hear daily at Meridian Advisory, and the answer is never one-size-fits-all. The right path depends on your financial goals, timeline, liquidity preferences, and long-term strategy.

In this guide, we'll break down both options across the most popular Citizenship by Investment programs so you can make an informed decision — or better yet, book a call with our senior advisor Rachel and get a recommendation tailored to your exact situation.

Understanding the Two Main Routes

The Donation Route (Non-Refundable Contribution)

The donation route — sometimes called the "fund contribution" or "government contribution" option — involves making a one-time, non-refundable payment to a government fund. Think of it as a direct transaction: you contribute capital, and in return, you receive citizenship.

Key characteristics:

  • Lower total cost in most programs
  • Faster processing timelines
  • No asset management responsibilities
  • Non-recoverable — the money is gone once contributed
  • Simplest path from a paperwork and compliance standpoint

The Real Estate Investment Route

The real estate route requires purchasing approved property — typically resort developments, hotel shares, or residential units — at or above a minimum investment threshold. You hold the property for a mandated period (usually 5–7 years), after which you can sell.

Key characteristics:

  • Higher upfront capital requirement
  • Potential for return on investment (rental income + resale value)
  • Property holding period of 3–7 years depending on jurisdiction
  • Additional costs: government fees, legal fees, property taxes, maintenance
  • More complex due diligence and paperwork

The 7 Factors That Should Drive Your Decision

1. 💰 Liquidity & Capital Allocation

Choose donation if: You want the lowest possible total expenditure and don't want capital tied up in a foreign asset for years. The donation is a sunk cost, but it's a known sunk cost — there are no surprises.

Choose real estate if: You have surplus capital, are comfortable with illiquid investments, and see value in holding a tangible asset in a growing market.

2. ⏱️ Timeline

Choose donation if: Speed is a priority. Donation applications are simpler to process because there's no need for property due diligence, title verification, or escrow arrangements.

Choose real estate if: You can afford to wait an additional 30–60 days for processing and are planning long-term rather than reacting to an urgent situation.

3. 📈 Return on Investment

Choose donation if: You've already done the math and you'd rather deploy that capital in your own business or portfolio where you can control returns. The "lost" donation amount is simply the cost of acquiring citizenship.

Choose real estate if: You believe in the appreciation potential of Caribbean or European resort properties and want the possibility — not the guarantee — of recovering some or all of your investment.

A word of caution: Many CBI-approved real estate projects are priced at a premium specifically because they're part of an approved program. The resale market for these properties can be limited, and rental yields may be lower than advertised. We've seen clients recover anywhere from 40% to 95% of their initial investment upon resale — rarely 100%.

4. 🧾 Tax & Estate Planning Implications

The tax treatment of a donation versus a real estate purchase varies significantly depending on your country of residence and tax obligations. In some jurisdictions, a real estate purchase may offer depreciation benefits. In others, it may create additional reporting obligations.

This is one of the strongest reasons to speak with an advisor before choosing. The wrong route could cost more in tax consequences than you save on the investment itself.

5. 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Size

For families with multiple dependents, the donation route often scales more favorably. Many programs charge a flat or marginally increased donation amount for families, while the real estate minimum remains fixed regardless of how many family members are included.

For a family of five, the donation route in St. Kitts, for example, may still be significantly less than the real estate minimum plus government and processing fees for each dependent.

6. 🌍 Long-Term Residency Intentions

Choose donation if: You want the passport but have no intention of living in or frequently visiting the country. A property you'll never use is a liability, not an asset.

Choose real estate if: You genuinely plan to spend time in the country — whether for business, retirement, or lifestyle. Owning property in Grenada or Portugal can be a legitimate part of your life plan, not just a citizenship vehicle.

7. 🔄 Exit Strategy

Choose donation if: You want a clean, simple transaction with no residual obligations. Once citizenship is granted, you're done.

Choose real estate if: You have a clear plan for the property after the holding period — resale to the next CBI applicant, conversion to a personal vacation home, or long-term rental income.

Common Mistakes We See

After advising hundreds of clients at Meridian Advisory, these are the most frequent errors people make when choosing between routes:

1. Choosing real estate purely to "get money back" — without factoring in total costs (legal fees, maintenance, insurance, property taxes, management fees, and potential depreciation). When you add everything up, the "recoverable" route sometimes costs more net than the donation.

2. Ignoring opportunity cost — $400,000 tied up in a Caribbean resort for seven years could have generated significant returns if invested elsewhere. Always compare the CBI real estate investment against your personal benchmark rate of return.

3. Skipping independent property due diligence — Not all "government-approved" projects are created equal. Some developments have stalled, underperformed, or been mismanaged. Always conduct independent due diligence beyond what the program requires.

4. Making the decision in isolation — Your CBI route should be part of a broader wealth, tax, and mobility strategy, not a standalone decision made on a Saturday afternoon.

So Which Should You Choose?

Here's the simplest framework we share with our clients:

> If your primary goal is citizenship at the lowest cost with the least complexity, choose the donation route.

>

> If your primary goal is citizenship PLUS a legitimate investment thesis — and you have the capital, patience, and risk tolerance to support it — consider the real estate route.

Most of our clients — particularly entrepreneurs, tech founders, and crypto investors — opt for the donation route. They'd rather deploy capital into their own ventures and treat the citizenship acquisition as an operational expense. It's clean, fast, and predictable.

But for clients with a genuine interest in Caribbean or European real estate, the investment route can be a smart dual-purpose play — when executed with proper guidance.
