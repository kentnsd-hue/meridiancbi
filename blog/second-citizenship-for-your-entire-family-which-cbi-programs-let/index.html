Second Citizenship for Your Entire Family: Which CBI Programs Let You Include Parents, Siblings, and Extended Relatives?

August 16, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

By Meridian Advisory | June 2026

When high-net-worth individuals explore citizenship by investment, the question almost always evolves beyond "Which passport is best for me?" to something far more personal:

"Can my family come with me?"

It's the right question to ask — and the answer varies dramatically depending on which program you choose. Some CBI programs define "family" narrowly as a spouse and minor children. Others take a remarkably inclusive approach, extending eligibility to parents, grandparents, siblings, and even adult children with no age cap.

Getting this wrong can cost you hundreds of thousands of dollars in separate applications. Getting it right means one investment covers the people who matter most.

Let's break it down.

Why Family Inclusion Matters More Than You Think

Before we dive into program specifics, let's quantify the stakes.

A single CBI application for one person might cost $100,000–$150,000 in government fees and investment. Adding a spouse and two children might bring the total to $175,000–$200,000. But if you need to file a separate application for an aging parent or an adult sibling? You could be looking at doubling your total outlay.

Programs with broad dependent eligibility aren't just more convenient — they're dramatically more cost-effective per family member.

Beyond cost, there's the strategic dimension. A second citizenship offers:

  • Shared access to visa-free travel across your family unit
  • Unified estate and succession planning across jurisdictions
  • A collective safety net — political instability, healthcare crises, and economic downturns don't affect individuals in isolation; they affect families

The bottom line: your CBI program should match the way your family actually works, not some narrow bureaucratic definition.

Program-by-Program Breakdown: Who Can You Include?

🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis — The Gold Standard for Family Inclusion

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse
  • Children of any age (biological, adopted, or stepchildren)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 55+
  • Siblings of the main applicant (unmarried, no children, under 30)

Why it stands out:

St. Kitts & Nevis operates the world's oldest CBI program (established 1984), and its maturity shows in its policies. The inclusion of siblings under 30 is a standout feature that very few programs replicate. For applicants with younger brothers or sisters who haven't yet established their own financial independence, this is an extraordinary opportunity.

The removal of the age cap on children is another major advantage. Many families include adult children in their 20s or 30s who are still financially dependent or pursuing education — St. Kitts accommodates this reality.

Minimum investment: $250,000 contribution to the Sustainable Island State Contribution (SISC) fund for a family of up to four.

Processing time: Typically 60–90 days.

🇬🇩 Grenada — Family-Friendly With a Powerful Bonus

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse
  • Children under 30 (if financially dependent or enrolled in higher education)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 55+
  • Siblings of the main applicant (unmarried, childless, under 18)

Why it stands out:

Grenada's real differentiator isn't just family inclusion — it's what the passport unlocks. Grenada is one of the few Caribbean CBI nations with an E-2 Treaty Investor Visa agreement with the United States. This means Grenadian citizens can apply for a renewable U.S. visa that allows them to live, work, and operate a business in the U.S.

For families with ambitions that include the American market, Grenada is often the strategic first move.

The sibling inclusion is more restrictive than St. Kitts (under 18 rather than under 30), but the parent and grandparent provisions are generous.

Minimum investment: $235,000 contribution to the National Transformation Fund (NTF) for a family of up to four.

Processing time: Approximately 90–120 days.

🇲🇹 Malta — European Prestige, Selective Family Definition

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse or partner in a long-term relationship
  • Children under 29 (if financially dependent)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 55+ (if financially dependent)

Why it stands out:

Malta's Exceptional Investor Naturalization (MEIN) program grants full European Union citizenship — arguably the most powerful citizenship status money can buy. With it comes the right to live, work, and study anywhere across 27 EU member states.

However, Malta does not include siblings, and its overall cost is significantly higher than Caribbean programs. The minimum investment pathway (via the 36-month residency track) requires a €600,000+ contribution plus real estate and philanthropic donations.

Family inclusion is solid for the nuclear family and aging parents, but if your priority is covering siblings or a larger extended family, Malta may need to be paired with a second program.

Minimum investment: €600,000 contribution (36-month track) or €750,000 (12-month track), plus property and donation requirements.

Processing time: 14–38 months depending on the track selected.

🇵🇹 Portugal Golden Visa — Residency First, Citizenship Later

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse or registered partner
  • Children (minor and adult dependent children)
  • Dependent parents of the applicant and the spouse (aged 55+ or 65+, depending on circumstances)
  • Siblings who are minors under the guardianship of the applicant

Why it stands out:

Portugal's Golden Visa is technically a residency-by-investment program rather than direct citizenship. However, it leads to permanent residency after five years and citizenship eligibility after five years — with Portugal's famously flexible physical presence requirements (as low as seven days per year on average).

For families, the standout feature is the inclusion of parents from both sides — the main applicant's parents and the spouse's parents. Very few programs offer this bilateral generosity.

The sibling provision is narrow (minors under guardianship), but for families with aging parents on both sides, Portugal is remarkably accommodating.

Important 2026 note: Portugal restructured its Golden Visa in recent years, eliminating residential real estate in major cities (Lisbon, Porto) as a qualifying route. Current qualifying investments focus on fund subscriptions (minimum €500,000), commercial real estate, and other approved categories. Always verify current qualifying routes before applying.

Minimum investment: €500,000 (investment fund route).

Processing time to residency: 12–18 months. Citizenship eligibility: ~5 years.

🇦🇬 Antigua & Barbuda — Built for Larger Families

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse
  • Children under 30 (if financially dependent)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 55+
  • Siblings of the main applicant (under 30, unmarried, no children)

Why it stands out:

Antigua's program is structured with larger families explicitly in mind. The government offers a discounted contribution rate for families of five or more, bringing the per-person cost down meaningfully for bigger households.

The sibling provision mirrors St. Kitts (under 30, unmarried, no children), making Antigua one of the most inclusive Caribbean programs.

Antigua also requires a minimum of five days of physical presence within the first five years — one of the lightest residency obligations of any CBI program globally.

Minimum investment: $230,000 contribution to the National Development Fund for a family of up to four; $240,000 for five or more.

Processing time: 90–120 days.

🇩🇲 Dominica — Affordable, With Reasonable Family Coverage

Eligible dependents:

  • Spouse
  • Children under 30 (if financially dependent or in full-time education)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 55+
  • Siblings aged 18–25 (unmarried, no children, financially dependent)

Why it stands out:

Dominica consistently ranks as the most affordable CBI program in the Caribbean, making it an excellent option for families who want broad coverage without the premium price tag.

The sibling inclusion (18–25) is more restrictive than St. Kitts or Antigua, but it exists — which already sets Dominica apart from most global programs.

Minimum investment: $200,000 contribution to the Economic Diversification Fund for a family of up to four.

Processing time: Approximately 90 days.

Strategic Considerations: How to Choose the Right Program for Your Family

1. Map Your Actual Family Structure First

Before comparing programs, write down every family member you'd want to include. Ages, marital statuses, financial dependency — all of it matters. The "cheapest" program on paper becomes the most expensive if it forces you to file two or three separate applications.

2. Think Generationally

CBI isn't just about your generation. A second passport is an asset that compounds over time. Your children will pass it to their children. Choose a jurisdiction that aligns with your family's long-term geographic and financial trajectory.

3. Consider Stacking Programs

Some families find that no single program covers everyone. A common strategy: use a Caribbean program (St. Kitts, Antigua) for broad family inclusion at lower cost, then pursue Malta or Portugal separately for the primary applicant who needs EU access. This "stacking" approach can be surprisingly cost-efficient.

4. Don't Overlook Processing Times

If you're pursuing CBI as part of a broader relocation or tax planning strategy, timing matters. Caribbean programs (60–120 days) move far faster than European options (12–38 months). For families in urgent situations — political instability, expiring tax windows — speed may override all other considerations.

Common Questions We Hear From Families

"My child just turned 30. Are they excluded?"

In most Caribbean programs, yes — with the notable exception of St. Kitts, which has removed the age cap on children entirely. In other cases, your child may need to file as a separate primary applicant.

"Can I add family members after my application is approved?"

Most programs allow you to add new dependents (newborns, new spouses) after citizenship is granted, though this involves additional fees and processing. It's almost always more cost-effective to include everyone in the initial application when possible.

"My parents aren't financially dependent on me. Can I still include them?"

This depends on the program. Some require demonstrated financial dependency; others simply require that parents or grandparents meet the age threshold. This is exactly the kind of nuance that makes professional advisory essential.

The Bottom Line

Citizenship by investment is rarely a solo decision. It touches spouses, children, aging parents, and sometimes siblings. The program you choose should reflect the full scope of your family — not just the applicant who signs the check.

The difference between the right program and the wrong one can mean hundreds of thousands of dollars and the difference between your mother having a second passport or not.

This is precisely why a conversation with an experienced advisor matters.
