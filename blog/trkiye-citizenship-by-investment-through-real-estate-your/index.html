Türkiye Citizenship by Investment Through Real Estate: Your Complete 2026 Guide

September 01, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Updated for 2026 | By Meridian Advisory

Türkiye has quietly become one of the most compelling citizenship-by-investment destinations in the world — and in 2026, the program continues to attract entrepreneurs, investors, and globally mobile professionals who want a powerful second passport backed by tangible real estate assets.

With its strategic position bridging Europe and Asia, a rapidly maturing economy, and one of the most straightforward CBI processes available, Türkiye deserves serious consideration from anyone building a global mobility strategy.

Here's everything you need to know about obtaining Turkish citizenship through real estate investment in 2026.

Why Türkiye? The Strategic Case

Before diving into program mechanics, it's worth understanding why Türkiye has emerged as a top-tier destination for second citizenship seekers:

  • Geopolitical positioning: Türkiye sits at the crossroads of Europe, the Middle East, and Central Asia — a genuine gateway economy with access to multiple markets.
  • NATO membership: As a NATO member state, Türkiye offers a layer of geopolitical stability and Western alignment that many CBI nations cannot match.
  • Visa-free travel: A Turkish passport provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 110+ countries and territories, including Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Thailand, and much of South America.
  • E-2 Treaty with the United States: This is a game-changer. Turkish citizens are eligible for the U.S. E-2 Investor Visa, which allows you to live and work in the United States. Very few CBI programs offer this pathway, making Türkiye one of the most practical "backdoor" routes to U.S. residency.
  • Growing economy: Türkiye boasts the 17th largest economy globally, with a GDP that has shown resilience despite inflationary pressures. The real estate market, particularly in Istanbul, Antalya, and the Aegean coast, has delivered strong capital appreciation in USD terms.

Program Overview: Real Estate Route to Turkish Citizenship

Minimum Investment Threshold

As of 2026, the minimum real estate investment required for Turkish citizenship is:

> $400,000 USD in real property

This threshold was raised from $250,000 in June 2022, reflecting the program's growing popularity and the Turkish government's desire to attract higher-caliber investment. Despite the increase, $400,000 remains one of the most accessible price points among citizenship programs that offer a real, tangible asset in return.

Key Requirements

| Requirement | Details |

|---|---|

| Minimum investment | $400,000 USD in real estate |

| Holding period | 3 years (property cannot be sold within this period) |

| Property type | Residential, commercial, or land |

| Number of properties | One or multiple properties (must total $400,000+) |

| Valuation | Must be appraised by a government-licensed valuer |

| Payment | Must be made via bank transfer (documented and traceable) |

| Family inclusion | Spouse and children under 18 are included |

| Residency requirement | None — you do not need to live in Türkiye |

| Language requirement | None |

What You Actually Get

  • Full Turkish citizenship — not residency, not a visa, but an actual passport
  • Inclusion of spouse and dependent children under 18
  • No requirement to renounce your existing citizenship (Türkiye allows dual citizenship)
  • Right to live, work, and study in Türkiye indefinitely
  • Access to the Turkish healthcare and education systems
  • Eligibility for the U.S. E-2 Investor Visa

The Process: Step by Step

Here's what the timeline typically looks like in 2026:

Step 1: Initial Consultation & Strategy (Week 1–2)

Work with an experienced advisory firm to define your goals, budget, and preferred property type and location. This is where you determine whether Türkiye is the right fit within your broader global mobility strategy.

Step 2: Property Selection & Due Diligence (Week 2–6)

Identify qualifying properties, conduct legal due diligence, verify title deeds, and obtain a government-approved property valuation (known as a tapu appraisal). This step is critical — errors here can derail an entire application.

Step 3: Property Purchase & Title Deed Transfer (Week 6–8)

Complete the purchase, transfer funds via bank wire, and register the property with the Land Registry Office (Tapu ve Kadastro Genel Müdürlüğü). A "not-for-sale" annotation is placed on the title deed for three years.

Step 4: Residency Permit Application (Week 8–10)

Apply for a short-term residency permit, which serves as a prerequisite for the citizenship application. This is largely a procedural step.

Step 5: Citizenship Application Submission (Week 10–12)

Submit the full citizenship application package to the General Directorate of Population and Citizenship Affairs. Documentation includes the title deed, valuation report, bank transfer receipts, passport copies, biometric photos, and family documentation.

Step 6: Review & Approval (Month 3–6)

The government reviews the application, conducts background checks, and issues a decision. In 2026, most straightforward applications are being processed within 3 to 6 months, though timelines can vary.

Step 7: Passport Issuance

Once approved, you and your family members receive Turkish ID cards and can apply for Turkish passports.

Total timeline: Approximately 4 to 8 months from start to passport.

Real Estate Market Snapshot: Where to Invest in 2026

Istanbul

The undisputed economic capital. Istanbul offers the deepest real estate market, the strongest rental yields (4–7% gross in prime areas), and the best liquidity when it comes time to sell after the three-year hold. Neighborhoods like Beşiktaş, Kadıköy, Şişli, and the emerging districts along the new metro lines continue to attract both domestic and international demand.

Antalya

Türkiye's Mediterranean jewel. Antalya has become a magnet for international buyers — particularly from Russia, the Middle East, and Central Asia. Strong tourism infrastructure means solid short-term rental potential, and property prices remain below Istanbul levels, giving investors more square footage per dollar.

Bodrum & The Aegean Coast

The luxury end of the Turkish market. Bodrum attracts a high-end crowd, and premium villas and boutique developments here can serve as both an investment and a personal retreat. Appreciation has been strong, though rental seasonality is more pronounced.

Ankara

Often overlooked, Türkiye's capital offers lower entry prices and steady demand driven by the government, university, and diplomatic sectors. It's a practical choice for investors focused purely on yield rather than lifestyle.

Emerging Markets: Mersin, Trabzon, Bursa

These secondary cities are gaining traction among savvy investors looking for earlier-stage appreciation. Government infrastructure investments and growing domestic migration are tailwinds worth watching.

Important Considerations & Due Diligence

Work With Licensed Professionals

The Turkish real estate market, while full of opportunity, also has pitfalls for uninformed buyers. Title deed irregularities, overvaluation, unlicensed developments, and misrepresented zoning can all create problems. Always work with:

  • A licensed Turkish attorney experienced in CBI transactions
  • A government-approved property appraiser
  • An experienced advisory firm that can coordinate the entire process

Tax Implications

Türkiye imposes a property acquisition tax (approximately 4% of the declared value), and there are annual property taxes that vary by municipality. Rental income is taxable in Türkiye, though rates are relatively modest. Critically, you should also consider the tax implications in your country of current tax residency — obtaining Turkish citizenship alone does not change your tax residency status.

Currency Considerations

Property prices are typically quoted in Turkish Lira but benchmarked to USD for CBI purposes. The Lira has experienced significant volatility in recent years, which has actually worked in favor of foreign-currency buyers who benefit from increased purchasing power. However, this dynamic also means that your investment's USD-denominated value will be influenced by exchange rate movements during the holding period.

After the Three-Year Hold

Once the three-year restriction period expires, you are free to sell the property. Your citizenship is permanent and is not revoked upon sale. This is a key distinction — you are making a time-limited investment commitment, not a permanent one.

Common Questions We Hear

"Do I need to visit Türkiye to complete the process?"

At least one visit is generally recommended for property inspection and biometric enrollment, though much of the process can be managed remotely through a power of attorney.

"Can I rent out the property during the three-year hold?"

Yes. You are free to generate rental income from the property during the holding period. Many of our clients structure their investments to produce yield while they wait out the restriction.

"What happens if property values decline?"

Your citizenship is not affected by changes in property value after purchase. The $400,000 threshold only needs to be met at the time of acquisition.

"Is the program at risk of being shut down?"

While no government program comes with a perpetual guarantee, the Turkish CBI program has strong institutional support and has been running since 2017. The threshold increase in 2022 signaled the government's intent to refine rather than eliminate the program.

"Can I include my parents in the application?"

Currently, the program covers the primary applicant, spouse, and children under 18. Parents are not included, though they may qualify for long-term residency through separate channels.

The Bottom Line

In 2026, Türkiye remains one of the most strategically valuable citizenship-by-investment programs available. It sits in a unique sweet spot: lower cost than European programs, backed by real assets, processed in months rather than years, and — crucially — it opens a door to the United States that very few other passports can.

It's not the right fit for everyone. If EU access is your primary goal, Portugal or Malta will serve you better. If passport strength is your sole metric, Caribbean programs may offer more visa-free destinations per dollar invested.

But if you want a tangible asset in one of the world's most dynamic economies, a pathway to U.S. residency, and a permanent second citizenship for your family — Türkiye deserves a place at the top of your shortlist.
