The Basics: How Türkiye's Citizenship by Real Estate Investment Works

Türkiye's Citizenship by Investment (CBI) program allows foreign nationals to obtain full Turkish citizenship — including a passport — by purchasing real estate valued at a minimum of $400,000 USD.

Here are the core requirements as of 2026:

  • Minimum Investment: $400,000 USD in real estate (up from the earlier $250,000 threshold)
  • Holding Period: The property must be held for a minimum of 3 years from the date of purchase and a restriction annotation is placed on the title deed
  • Eligible Property Types: Residential, commercial, or land — purchased from a Turkish citizen or a Turkish legal entity
  • Family Inclusion: Spouse and children under 18 are included in the application at no additional investment
  • Processing Timeline: Approximately 3 to 6 months from application to passport issuance, though timelines can vary
  • No Residency Requirement: You do not need to live in Türkiye to obtain or maintain citizenship

> Key Point: The property must be appraised and the transaction must go through an officially licensed valuation firm. The purchase price on the title deed must meet or exceed the $400,000 threshold as confirmed by the appraisal report.

The Turkish Passport: What Does It Actually Get You?

This is where the conversation gets interesting. The Turkish passport in 2026 offers:

🌍 Visa-Free & Visa-on-Arrival Access

The Turkish passport currently provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 110+ countries and territories, including:

  • Japan
  • South Korea
  • Singapore
  • Thailand
  • Most of South America (Brazil, Argentina, Chile)
  • Qatar, Bahrain, and other Gulf states
  • Much of Central Asia and the Caribbean

🇺🇸 E-2 Treaty Investor Visa Eligibility (United States)

This is arguably the single biggest draw for many investors. Türkiye has a bilateral E-2 Treaty with the United States, meaning Turkish passport holders can apply for the E-2 Investor Visa — a renewable, long-term visa that allows you to live, work, and operate a business in the U.S.

For entrepreneurs and investors who want a pathway to the American market without the EB-5 price tag or green card lottery, this is a game-changer.

🏦 Access to Turkish Banking and Financial Markets

Turkish citizenship opens the door to full banking access, investment accounts, and participation in Türkiye's growing real estate and capital markets — a significant advantage for those looking to diversify internationally.

🔄 Dual Citizenship Permitted

Türkiye allows dual citizenship, meaning you do not have to renounce your existing nationality.

The Real Estate Market in Türkiye: 2026 Snapshot

Türkiye's real estate market has undergone significant shifts in recent years, driven by currency fluctuations, inflation adjustments, and surging foreign demand. Here's what the landscape looks like heading into mid-2026:

Istanbul

Still the crown jewel. Istanbul accounts for the largest share of foreign property purchases. Key districts for CBI investors include:

  • Beyoğlu & Şişli — Central, high rental yield potential
  • Başakşehir & Esenyurt — More affordable, popular with Gulf and Asian investors
  • Kadıköy & Üsküdar (Asian side) — Growing demand, lifestyle appeal
  • Ataşehir & Maltepe — Modern developments with strong capital appreciation potential

Average prices in prime Istanbul neighborhoods range from approximately $1,500 to $4,000+ per square meter, depending on the district and development.

Antalya

Türkiye's Mediterranean coast remains a favorite for lifestyle-driven investors. Antalya offers strong short-term rental yields, particularly in areas like Konyaaltı, Lara, and Kalkan.

Ankara

The capital offers lower entry prices and appeals to investors looking for commercial real estate opportunities near government and institutional centers.

Bodrum & Izmir

Luxury coastal markets with growing international appeal. Bodrum in particular has seen a wave of high-end villa developments targeting the $500K–$1M+ segment.

Important Market Considerations for 2026:

  • Currency dynamics: The Turkish Lira has experienced considerable volatility. For USD, EUR, or GBP holders, this can mean significant purchasing power — but it also requires careful structuring of the transaction.
  • Appraisal vigilance: Ensure your property's appraisal aligns with the actual market value. Authorities have increased scrutiny on over-valued appraisals used to meet the $400,000 threshold artificially.
  • Rental income potential: Depending on the location and type of property, gross rental yields in Türkiye range from approximately 4% to 8% annually — making this a genuine investment, not just a passport play.

Step-by-Step: The 2026 Application Process

Here's a simplified breakdown of the journey from decision to passport:

Step 1: Consultation & Strategy

Work with an experienced CBI advisory firm (like Meridian Advisory) to determine if Türkiye aligns with your mobility, tax, and investment goals. We help you define the right property type, location, and structure.

Step 2: Property Selection & Due Diligence

Identify suitable properties. This includes:

  • Licensed property appraisal (SPK-approved valuation firm)
  • Title deed (TAPU) verification
  • Confirmation that the seller is a Turkish national or Turkish legal entity
  • Ensuring the property has no existing liens, debts, or encumbrances

Step 3: Property Purchase & Title Deed Transfer

Complete the purchase, transfer the title deed at the Land Registry Office (Tapu Müdürlüğü), and have the 3-year sale restriction annotation placed on the deed.

Step 4: Conformity Certificate

Obtain a Certificate of Conformity from the relevant government authority (Ministry of Environment, Urbanization, and Climate Change), confirming your investment meets the program requirements.

Step 5: Residence Permit Application

Apply for a short-term residence permit, which serves as the administrative bridge to your citizenship application.

Step 6: Citizenship Application

Submit your citizenship application to the General Directorate of Population and Citizenship Affairs (Nüfus ve Vatandaşlık İşleri Genel Müdürlüğü) along with all required documentation.

Step 7: Approval & Passport Issuance

Upon approval, you and your eligible family members receive Turkish citizenship and can apply for your Turkish passports.

Total timeline: Approximately 3–6 months, though complex cases or documentation issues can extend this.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Based on cases we've reviewed and clients we've assisted, here are pitfalls to watch out for:

1. Buying from a foreign-owned entity. The seller must be a Turkish citizen or Turkish company. Purchases from foreign nationals do not qualify.

2. Relying on inflated appraisals. Turkish authorities have cracked down on artificial over-valuations. If the appraisal doesn't reflect genuine market value, your application could be delayed or rejected.

3. Not verifying title deed status. Always conduct a thorough title deed check for liens, mortgage encumbrances, or legal disputes before purchasing.

4. Skipping professional advisory. The process involves Turkish legal, tax, and immigration frameworks that require specialized knowledge. Working without experienced guidance increases risk significantly.

5. Ignoring the 3-year holding commitment. Selling before the 3-year annotation period expires can result in revocation of citizenship.

Tax Considerations

Turkish citizenship does carry tax implications that should be understood upfront:

  • Tax residency ≠ Citizenship. Simply obtaining Turkish citizenship does not automatically make you a Turkish tax resident. Tax residency is generally triggered by spending more than 6 months in Türkiye within a calendar year.
  • Rental income tax. If you rent out your property, rental income earned in Türkiye is subject to Turkish income tax. However, exemptions and deductions may apply.
  • Capital gains tax. Properties sold within 5 years of purchase may be subject to capital gains tax. Properties held longer than 5 years are generally exempt.
  • No global wealth tax. Türkiye does not impose a wealth tax on global assets.
