What Happens During CBI Due Diligence — And How to Prepare for It

July 27, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

A transparent look at the background checks, documentation requirements, and timelines that stand between you and your second passport.

Published by Meridian Advisory | June 2026

If you're seriously exploring citizenship by investment, you've probably heard the phrase "due diligence" mentioned more than once. It's the part of the process that makes or breaks applications — and yet, it remains one of the most misunderstood stages for first-time applicants.

Let's change that.

At Meridian Advisory, we believe informed clients make better decisions. Here's a comprehensive breakdown of what CBI due diligence actually involves, what the Citizenship by Investment Units (CIUs) are looking for, and exactly how you can prepare to ensure a smooth, successful application.

Why Due Diligence Exists — And Why It's Getting Stricter

Citizenship by investment programs exist at the intersection of sovereignty, economics, and international security. Governments offering these programs are essentially extending the rights and protections of their nation to foreign nationals. That's not something any responsible country takes lightly.

In recent years — particularly following pressure from the EU, the OECD, and the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) — due diligence standards across the Caribbean and Europe have tightened significantly. Programs that were once criticized for lax vetting have invested heavily in multi-layered screening processes.

As of 2026, the most reputable CBI programs operate what's known as a four-tier due diligence framework:

1. Government-level screening by the CIU itself

2. Independent third-party investigation by international due diligence firms

3. International database checks against sanctions lists, Interpol, and financial crime databases

4. Regional coordination, particularly among Caribbean CBI nations sharing intelligence

This isn't a rubber stamp. It's a rigorous process — and that's actually a good thing. The integrity of these programs is what protects the value of your new citizenship.

What Exactly Gets Checked?

Let's break this down category by category.

1. Identity Verification

This is the foundational layer. Authorities will verify that you are who you say you are.

What they examine:

  • Passport validity, issuance history, and any previous name changes
  • National ID documents
  • Birth certificates
  • Marriage and divorce certificates (if applicable)
  • Consistency across all submitted documents

What to know: Discrepancies — even innocent ones, like a name spelled differently across two documents — can trigger delays. If your name has ever been transliterated from a non-Latin script (Arabic, Chinese, Cyrillic, etc.), prepare explanatory documentation in advance.

2. Criminal Background Checks

Every reputable CBI program runs comprehensive criminal screenings.

What they examine:

  • Police clearance certificates from your country of citizenship, country of residence, and any country where you've lived for more than one year in the past decade
  • Interpol databases (including Red and Yellow Notices)
  • National and regional law enforcement databases
  • Court records in relevant jurisdictions

What to know: A criminal record doesn't automatically disqualify you, depending on the nature and age of the offense. Minor infractions from years ago may not be an issue. Serious financial crimes, fraud, or anything terrorism-related is an absolute disqualifier. Full disclosure is always better than non-disclosure — concealing information that later surfaces is one of the fastest ways to get rejected.

3. Financial Background and Source of Funds

This is where the process gets granular, and where many underprepared applicants run into trouble.

What they examine:

  • Proof of legitimate source of funds for the investment (donation or real estate purchase)
  • Bank statements (typically 12–24 months)
  • Business ownership records, audited financial statements, and corporate filings
  • Tax returns and proof of tax compliance in your country of residence
  • Employment verification and salary history (for salaried applicants)
  • Source of wealth documentation — how you accumulated your net worth over time

What to know: "Source of funds" and "source of wealth" are two different things. Source of funds refers to where the specific money for the CBI investment is coming from — a particular bank account, a recent business sale, liquidated investments, etc. Source of wealth is the broader narrative of how you built your financial position over your career. You'll need to address both.

If your wealth comes from cryptocurrency, be prepared to provide wallet histories, exchange records, and potentially a trail going back to initial acquisition. Crypto-derived wealth isn't a red flag per se, but it does require thorough documentation since blockchain-based assets have historically attracted additional scrutiny.

4. Professional and Business Background

CIUs want to understand what you do and how you do it.

What they examine:

  • Current and historical business affiliations
  • Directorships, shareholdings, and beneficial ownership in companies
  • Industry-specific compliance (especially for applicants in financial services, defense, mining, pharmaceuticals, or government contracting)
  • Professional licenses and regulatory standing
  • Media and adverse press screening

What to know: Adverse media screening is more sophisticated than most applicants realize. Due diligence firms use AI-powered tools that scan global media sources in multiple languages. An old news article about a lawsuit, a regulatory investigation, or even a contentious business dispute can surface. If there's something out there, your advisory team needs to know about it before the CIU finds it. Context and documentation are your best defense.

5. Political Exposure (PEP Screening)

Politically Exposed Persons — and their close associates and family members — face a higher standard of scrutiny.

What they examine:

  • Current or former government positions
  • Senior roles in state-owned enterprises
  • Positions in political parties, international organizations, or the judiciary
  • Family connections to PEPs (spouse, children, parents, siblings)
  • Close business associates of PEPs

What to know: Being a PEP doesn't disqualify you, but it does trigger Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD). This means longer processing times, more documentation requests, and a higher bar for source-of-wealth verification. If you fall into this category, working with an experienced advisory firm isn't just helpful — it's practically essential.

6. International Sanctions and Watchlist Screening

This is non-negotiable across every program.

What they check against:

  • OFAC (U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control) Specially Designated Nationals List
  • EU Consolidated Sanctions List
  • UN Security Council Sanctions Lists
  • HM Treasury Sanctions List (UK)
  • FATF High-Risk and Non-Cooperative Jurisdictions lists
  • Country-specific sanctions databases

What to know: If you appear on any of these lists, your application will be denied. There are no exceptions. If you share a name with someone on a watchlist (more common than you'd think), prepare identifying documentation that clearly differentiates you.

7. Health and Character Checks

Some programs require medical examinations and/or character references.

What they examine:

  • Medical certificates (HIV testing is required by some Caribbean programs)
  • Character references from professionals (attorneys, accountants, bankers)
  • Personal declarations and sworn affidavits

Program-by-Program: How Due Diligence Intensity Varies

Not all CBI programs apply the same level of scrutiny. Here's a general comparison as of 2026:

| Program | Due Diligence Intensity | Typical Processing Time | Third-Party DD Firm |

|---|---|---|---|

| St. Kitts & Nevis | Very High | 60–90 days | Multiple firms used |

| Grenada | Very High | 90–120 days | Regional & international |

| Malta | Highest (EU standard) | 12–14 months | Extensive, multi-layered |

| Portugal Golden Visa | High (EU standard) | 6–12+ months | National security services |

| Dominica | High | 60–90 days | International DD firms |

| Antigua & Barbuda | High | 90–120 days | International DD firms |

Malta and Portugal, as EU member states, operate under European regulatory frameworks that add additional layers of oversight, including Europol and Schengen Information System checks.

The 10 Most Common Reasons Applications Get Delayed or Denied

Based on our experience at Meridian Advisory, these are the issues that cause the most problems:

1. Incomplete documentation — Missing a single required document can stall an application for weeks.

2. Inconsistent information — Details on your application form don't match supporting documents.

3. Undisclosed criminal history — Even a dismissed charge that you forgot to mention.

4. Insufficient source-of-funds documentation — Especially common with inherited wealth and crypto gains.

5. Adverse media hits — An old article you forgot about, or didn't know existed.

6. PEP status not properly addressed — Failing to proactively provide enhanced documentation.

7. Sanctioned country connections — Business dealings in or financial flows from high-risk jurisdictions.

8. Name-matching issues — Sharing a name with a flagged individual without providing disambiguation.

9. Outdated documents — Police clearances and medical certificates have expiration windows (typically 6 months).

10. Using unregistered or inexperienced agents — This one is entirely avoidable.

How to Prepare: A Practical Checklist

If you're considering a CBI application in 2026, here's how to set yourself up for success.

3–6 Months Before You Apply

  • [ ] Conduct a self-audit. Google yourself. Check what appears in English and any other languages relevant to your background. If there's adverse media, prepare a written explanation with supporting evidence.
  • [ ] Organize your financial records. Gather 24 months of bank statements, tax returns, business financials, and proof of asset ownership. The more organized this is upfront, the smoother the process.
  • [ ] Address any outstanding legal matters. Pending lawsuits, unresolved regulatory issues, or outstanding tax obligations should be resolved or documented before you apply.
  • [ ] Collect police clearance certificates. Request these from every country where you've resided for 12+ months in the past decade. Processing times vary — some countries take weeks.
  • [ ] Standardize your name across documents. If there are variations, prepare a statutory declaration or notarized letter explaining the discrepancies.

1–2 Months Before You Apply

  • [ ] Choose the right program for your profile. Not every program is ideal for every applicant. Your nationality, business background, travel needs, and financial situation all matter.
  • [ ] Engage a licensed, reputable advisory firm. This is not the time to cut corners. Your advisor should be a registered agent with the specific CBI program you're applying to.
  • [ ] Complete medical examinations. Schedule these so results are fresh when your application is submitted.
  • [ ] Prepare your source-of-wealth narrative. Write a clear, chronological explanation of how you built your wealth — from early career through to today. Support it with documentation at every stage.
  • [ ] Brief your references. Character references may be contacted. Make sure your referees know to expect outreach and can speak credibly to your character and professional standing.

At the Time of Application

  • [ ] Review every document for accuracy and consistency.
  • [ ] Ensure all documents are properly notarized, apostilled, or legalized per program requirements.
  • [ ] Provide certified translations for any documents not in English (or the program's official language).
  • [ ] Disclose everything. If in doubt, disclose. It's always better to over-share and provide context than to have something surface during the investigation that you didn't mention.
