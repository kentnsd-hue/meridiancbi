Portugal Golden Visa Through Investment Funds: Your Complete 2026 Guide

October 25, 2026 | Meridian Advisory

Published by Meridian Advisory | Updated June 2026

The Portugal Golden Visa Is Still Alive — But the Rules Have Changed

If you've been following European residency-by-investment news, you've likely heard conflicting reports about the Portugal Golden Visa. Let us set the record straight: the Portugal Golden Visa program is very much active in 2026, but the landscape looks dramatically different from just a few years ago.

Since the landmark legislative changes that removed real estate as a qualifying investment route, one pathway has emerged as the clear frontrunner for savvy investors:

Investment funds.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through everything you need to know about obtaining Portuguese residency — and eventually EU citizenship — through qualifying investment funds in 2026.

What Is the Portugal Golden Visa?

The Portugal Golden Visa (formally the Autorização de Residência para Atividade de Investimento, or ARI) is a residency-by-investment program that grants non-EU/EEA nationals legal residency in Portugal in exchange for a qualifying investment.

Why it remains one of the most coveted programs globally:

  • 🇪🇺 Gateway to the entire European Union — live, work, and travel freely across 27 EU member states
  • 🛂 Path to Portuguese citizenship after 5 years, granting one of the world's most powerful passports (ranking consistently in the top 5 globally)
  • 🏠 Minimal physical presence requirement — only 7 days per year on average
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family inclusion — spouse, dependent children, and even dependent parents can be added
  • 💰 Access to Portugal's Non-Habitual Residency (NHR) successor tax regime for potential tax optimization

Why Investment Funds Are Now the Primary Route

When Portugal eliminated direct real estate purchases from the Golden Visa pathway, it didn't kill the program — it refined it. The government's intent was clear: redirect foreign capital toward productive economic activity, innovation, and job creation rather than inflating property prices in Lisbon and Porto.

In 2026, the qualifying investment fund route has become the most popular and practical option. Here's why:

The Minimum Investment

€500,000 invested into a qualifying Portuguese investment fund.

This threshold has remained stable and represents one of the most competitive entry points for EU residency when you consider what you're getting in return.

How the Investment Fund Route Works: Step by Step

1. Choose a Qualifying Fund

Not just any fund qualifies. Under the current regulations, the investment fund must meet specific criteria:

  • Registered with the CMVM (Comissão do Mercado de Valores Mobiliários — Portugal's securities market regulator)
  • Minimum 60% of the fund's capital must be invested in Portuguese-based companies or assets
  • The fund cannot be invested in real estate (this is a critical distinction in 2026)
  • Minimum maturity period of 5 years — aligning with the citizenship eligibility timeline
  • Managed by a licensed Portuguese fund manager

2. Make the Investment

Once you've selected your fund (more on due diligence below), you transfer the €500,000 minimum investment. The capital is professionally managed throughout the holding period.

3. Submit Your Golden Visa Application

With proof of investment, you apply through AIMA (the Agency for Integration, Migration, and Asylum) — the entity that replaced SEF in managing immigration matters.

Required documentation typically includes:

  • Valid passport
  • Proof of investment (fund subscription confirmation)
  • Criminal background check (from country of origin and any country of residence)
  • Portuguese tax number (NIF)
  • Health insurance valid in Portugal
  • Proof of funds (demonstrating the investment capital is from legitimate sources)
  • Declaration of compliance with minimum stay requirements

4. Biometrics & Residency Card

After approval, you'll attend a biometrics appointment in Portugal and receive your residency card, typically valid for 2 years initially.

5. Renewals

The Golden Visa is renewed at the following intervals:

  • Initial permit: 2 years
  • First renewal: 2 years
  • Second renewal: 1 year (or apply directly for permanent residency/citizenship)

During each period, you must maintain the investment and meet the minimum stay requirement of 7 days in the first year and 14 days in each subsequent two-year period.

6. Citizenship at Year 5

After 5 years of legal residency, you become eligible to apply for Portuguese citizenship. This requires:

  • Maintained investment throughout
  • Met minimum stay requirements
  • Basic Portuguese language proficiency (A2 level — achievable with moderate study)
  • Clean criminal record
  • Ties to the Portuguese community (demonstrated through the above)

Fund Types Available in 2026

The Portuguese investment fund ecosystem has matured significantly. Here are the primary categories of qualifying funds investors are choosing this year:

🚀 Venture Capital Funds

Investing in Portuguese startups and scale-ups, particularly in Lisbon's booming tech ecosystem. Higher risk, higher potential return. Portugal has quietly become one of Europe's most dynamic tech hubs, and these funds offer exposure to that growth.

💼 Private Equity Funds

Targeting established Portuguese businesses across sectors — manufacturing, renewable energy, healthcare, agritech, and tourism infrastructure. Moderate risk profile with potential for meaningful returns.

🌱 ESG & Sustainability Funds

A growing category in 2026, these funds direct capital toward Portugal's green transition — solar energy, sustainable agriculture, electric mobility, and circular economy initiatives. Increasingly popular with investors who want their capital aligned with impact goals.

📊 Balanced / Diversified Funds

Spreading investments across multiple asset classes within Portugal — combining equity stakes, corporate debt, and other instruments. Generally the most conservative option within the qualifying fund universe.

Due Diligence: How to Choose the Right Fund

This is where most investors need expert guidance. Choosing the wrong fund can mean lost capital, regulatory complications, or even a rejected Golden Visa application. Here's what to evaluate:

Track Record of the Fund Manager

  • How long has the management company been operating?
  • What is their assets-under-management (AUM) history?
  • Have they managed Golden Visa-qualifying funds before?

Fee Structure

Look carefully at:

  • Management fees (typically 1.5–2.5% annually)
  • Performance fees (often 15–20% above a hurdle rate)
  • Subscription and redemption fees
  • Hidden costs that erode returns

Underlying Investment Strategy

  • Is the investment thesis sound and well-documented?
  • What sectors and companies are being targeted?
  • What is the expected return profile, and is it realistic?

Liquidity & Exit Terms

  • What happens at the 5-year mark?
  • Are there provisions for early exit (and at what cost)?
  • What is the fund's planned wind-down process?

CMVM Compliance

  • Is the fund fully registered and in good standing with the CMVM?
  • Has it passed all regulatory audits?

Alignment with Golden Visa Requirements

  • Does the fund explicitly confirm its eligibility for Golden Visa purposes?
  • Is the 60% Portugal-allocation rule being met and documented?

> ⚠️ A word of caution: The growth in Golden Visa fund demand has attracted some operators more interested in management fees than investor returns. Independent due diligence is not optional — it's essential. This is exactly where working with experienced advisors pays for itself many times over.

The Financial Reality: Costs Beyond the Investment

Let's be transparent about the full financial picture. Beyond the €500,000 fund investment, budget for:

| Cost Category | Estimated Amount |

|---|---|

| Government application fees | ~€5,500 per applicant + €533 per card |

| Legal fees | €10,000–€20,000 |

| Fund subscription costs | Varies by fund |

| Portuguese tax number (NIF) setup | €200–€500 |

| Document translation & apostille | €1,000–€2,500 |

| Health insurance | €500–€1,500/year |

| Renewal fees (per cycle) | ~€2,700 |

| Citizenship application (Year 5) | ~€250 |

Total all-in cost estimate (excluding the investment itself): approximately €20,000–€30,000 for a single applicant over the 5-year period.

Family members added to the application incur additional government fees but share the same underlying investment.

Common Questions We Hear in 2026

"Can I still buy property in Portugal for the Golden Visa?"

No. Direct real estate purchases were removed as a qualifying route. However, some investment funds do invest in commercial real estate or real estate-adjacent sectors — though the fund itself cannot be classified as a real estate fund for Golden Visa purposes. The nuances here matter, and incorrect structuring can disqualify your application.

"Is my investment at risk?"

Yes. Investment funds carry inherent market risk. Your €500,000 is not a government-guaranteed deposit. Returns depend on the fund's performance. This is precisely why fund selection and due diligence are critical.

"Do I need to move to Portugal?"

No. The Golden Visa's minimal stay requirement (averaging 7 days per year) is one of its greatest advantages. You can maintain your primary residence anywhere in the world.

"Can I work in Portugal with a Golden Visa?"

Yes. The Golden Visa grants full residency rights, including the right to work and do business in Portugal — and to travel freely throughout the Schengen Area.

"What about taxes?"

Portuguese tax residency is determined by spending 183+ days in the country, not by holding a Golden Visa. If you maintain less than 183 days of physical presence, you generally will not become a Portuguese tax resident. However, tax situations are highly individual — always consult a qualified tax advisor for your specific circumstances.

"What happens if the program is closed while I'm in the process?"

Historically, applicants who have submitted their applications before any policy change are grandfathered under the existing rules. However, this is never guaranteed for future changes, which is why acting decisively matters.

The 2026 Outlook: Why Act Now?

Several factors make 2026 a pivotal year for the Portugal Golden Visa:

1. Political uncertainty persists. There is ongoing political debate in Portugal about the future of the program. While it remains active today, there are no guarantees about tomorrow.

2. Processing times are improving. Following the transition from SEF to AIMA, backlogs that plagued 2024-2025 are being cleared. Applicants submitting in 2026 are seeing more predictable timelines.

3. The fund ecosystem is maturing. More high-quality, well-regulated funds are entering the market, giving investors better options than ever before. But this also means more noise to cut through.

4. Geopolitical demand is surging. Global uncertainty continues to drive demand for Plan B residency and citizenship among entrepreneurs and investors worldwide. Program capacity isn't infinite.

5. The 5-year clock starts when you apply. Every month you delay is a month later you become eligible for EU citizenship.
